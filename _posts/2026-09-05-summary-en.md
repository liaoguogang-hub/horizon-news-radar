## [Anthropic AI Formalizes Fermat&\#x27;s Last Theorem in Lean](https://www.anthropic.com/research/formalizing-fermats-last-theorem) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Anthropic has formally verified Fermat&\#x27;s Last Theorem in the Lean proof assistant using AI, producing approximately 13 million lines of Lean code. The proof follows the Darmon–Diamond–Taylor 1995 exposition of the Wiles–Taylor–Wiles argument, routed through the Langlands–Tunnell theorem and Ribet&\#x27;s level-lowering theorem, rather than the more modern proof based on Khare–Wiles–Taylor ideas. This milestone demonstrates that AI can formalize deeply complex mathematical proofs that have resisted automation for decades, potentially transforming mathematical refereeing and error detection. It also points toward broader applications in software verification, compiler correctness, OS kernel verification, and chip design with formal equivalence checking. The proof spans roughly 13 million lines of Lean code, raising natural questions about how to audit such a massive formal artifact for correctness. Anthropic structured the task in stages, and the choice of the Darmon–Diamond–Taylor exposition \(rather than Kevin Buzzard&\#x27;s ongoing modern formalization effort\) was deliberate — that modern proof has not yet been completed by humans in Lean.

hackernews · Hacker News \(热门\) · Sep 4, 18:42 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49568506)

**Background**: Fermat&\#x27;s Last Theorem, formulated by Pierre de Fermat in 1637, states that no three positive integers a, b, c can satisfy a^n + b^n = c^n for any integer n greater than two. It remained one of mathematics&\#x27; most famous unsolved problems until Andrew Wiles proved it in 1995 via the modularity theorem for semistable elliptic curves, combined with Ribet&\#x27;s theorem. Lean is an open-source interactive theorem prover and programming language that allows mathematicians and engineers to write proofs whose correctness is mechanically checked, distinguishing formal verification from traditional informal mathematical writing.

## [First-in-Human Study: Pig Kidney Xenotransplant as Bridge to Allotransplantation](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736%2826%2901295-X/fulltext?rss=yes) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Leonardo Riella and colleagues report the first-in-human case in which a genetically engineered porcine kidney functioned for 271 days—the longest dialysis-free xenograft survival to date—before being explanted and successfully replaced with a deceased-donor human kidney allotransplant. With thousands of patients dying annually while waiting for a human kidney, this proof-of-concept demonstrates that porcine xenografts can buy critically ill patients meaningful time without sensitizing them against future human transplants or transmitting porcine infections, potentially transforming the organ-shortage crisis. Early cellular rejection responded to treatment, but later graft failure was driven by microvascular injury progressing to thrombotic microangiopathy despite a negative donor-specific crossmatch—suggesting non-conventional antibody-mediated rejection mechanisms. Importantly, no clinically significant allosensitization or zoonotic infection was observed after 271 days.

rss · The Lancet · 最新文章 · Sep 3, 22:30

**Background**: Xenotransplantation—the transplantation of organs across species—has been pursued for decades as a solution to the chronic shortage of human donor organs. Recent advances in CRISPR-based gene editing have enabled the creation of pigs with multiple genetic modifications \(often 10 or more edits\) designed to reduce immune rejection and prevent zoonotic viral transmission. Donor-specific crossmatch testing and HLA sensitization \(allosensitization\) are standard tools for assessing compatibility in human kidney transplantation; a positive crossmatch or pre-existing sensitization increases the risk of antibody-mediated rejection and graft failure. Thrombotic microangiopathy \(TMA\) is a serious complication involving clot formation in small blood vessels, increasingly recognized in pig-to-human kidney xenotransplantation despite negative crossmatches, and may involve complement activation beyond conventional pathways.

## [Trusting-Trust Attack Extended to Entire Linux Distributions via strip Utility](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24888) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A research paper \(arXiv:2607.24888\) demonstrates that Ken Thompson&\#x27;s classic trusting-trust attack — long thought to apply only to compilers — can be extended to the \`strip\` utility, allowing a compromised build tool to backdoor an entire Linux distribution and propagate the backdoor through subsequent rebuilds. This research significantly broadens the known threat surface for software supply chain attacks, showing that any tool in the build pipeline — not just compilers — can serve as a vehicle for persistent, self-reproducing backdoors. It has major implications for how Linux distributions and other software projects must think about verifying the integrity of every tool in their build chains. The attack exploits the fact that \`strip\`, a standard binary utility used to remove symbol tables and debugging information from executables, is invoked during the build process of nearly every compiled binary in a distribution. The paper shows that a compromised \`strip\` can inject malicious code into binaries it processes and reproduce itself when those binaries \(including the compiler or \`strip\` itself\) are rebuilt from source.

rss · Lobsters \(技术社区\) · Sep 5, 10:58

**Background**: Ken Thompson&\#x27;s &\#x27;Reflections on Trusting Trust,&\#x27; presented in his 1984 Turing Award lecture, described a theoretical attack in which a compromised compiler inserts a backdoor into the programs it compiles — and crucially, reproduces that backdoor when the compiler itself is recompiled, making the compromise nearly invisible to source-code audits. The attack was long considered a theoretical curiosity specific to compilers. The \`strip\` utility is a standard GNU/Linux binary tool present in the GNU Binutils package, used in virtually every Linux distribution&\#x27;s build process to reduce binary size by removing symbols not needed at runtime. Recent real-world supply chain attacks, such as the 2024 XZ Utils backdoor \(CVE-2024-3094\), have shown that attackers are willing to invest years of effort to compromise widely-used open-source components.

## [Can AI Design Circuit Boards Yet? Practitioners Report Mixed Results](https://eebench.org/blog/can-ai-design-circuit-boards-yet/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Experienced PCB designers tested AI tools \(including Claude, Codex, and custom MCP servers\) on real circuit board projects and found that while AI can produce mostly functional designs, it consistently makes mechanical and routing errors—such as missing through-holes on component footprints and incorrect pad sizing—that still require human review and correction. PCB design is a high-stakes, multi-stage discipline where errors translate directly into costly board respins or hardware failures. Understanding where AI currently succeeds and fails helps hardware teams decide whether to integrate AI-assisted workflows now or wait, and signals which parts of the EDA toolchain are closest to automation. Tested AI workflows include the KiCAD MCP Server paired with Codex for flex PCB generation that passes DRC checks at JLC and PCBWay, and Claude Opus 4.8 designing a VGA signal generator using only 74-series logic and GALs for under $6 in fabrication cost. Common failure modes include missed through-holes, undersized center pads, and subtle signal-integrity issues—problems that DRC tools sometimes miss but that a human reviewer can typically fix with minor rework or blue-wire patches.

hackernews · Hacker News \(热门\) · Sep 4, 19:48 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49569366)

**Background**: PCB \(printed circuit board\) design traditionally follows a multi-step workflow: schematic capture, component footprint selection, board layout and routing, design rule checking \(DRC\), and fabrication file generation. Tools like KiCAD, Altium, and EAGLE dominate this space. Recently, AI coding assistants such as Claude and GPT-based models have been extended to hardware domains through MCP \(Model Context Protocol\) servers that let LLMs interface directly with EDA software. The question of whether these models can autonomously produce fabrication-ready boards is important because PCB errors are expensive to fix once a board is manufactured and assembled.

Mullvad VPN is a Sweden-based privacy-focused VPN provider, and Mullvad Browser is a privacy-hardened web browser developed in collaboration with the Tor Project.
