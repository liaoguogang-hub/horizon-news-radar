## [Anthropic Formalizes Fermat&\#x27;s Last Theorem in Lean](https://www.anthropic.com/research/formalizing-fermats-last-theorem) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Anthropic announced the formalization of Fermat&\#x27;s Last Theorem using AI and the Lean proof assistant, producing approximately 13 million lines of formally verified code. The proof follows the Darmon–Diamond–Taylor 1995 exposition of the Wiles–Taylor–Wiles argument, via the Langlands–Tunnell and Ribet&\#x27;s level-lowering theorems, rather than the modern Wiles-style proof. This landmark demonstration shows that LLMs can now formalize proofs of deeply complex mathematical results at scale, opening the door to AI-assisted verification of compilers, operating system kernels, and chip designs. It also suggests a future where mathematical papers could be formally checked automatically, reducing the refereeing burden and potentially catching errors in published proofs. The formalization totals about 13 million lines of Lean code, an unprecedented scale for a single mathematical result. Anthropic chose the Darmon–Diamond–Taylor exposition rather than the modern proof that Kevin Buzzard had been formalizing, meaning this effort runs in parallel with, rather than replaces, existing community formalization work.

hackernews · Hacker News \(热门\) · Sep 4, 18:42 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49568506)

**Background**: Fermat&\#x27;s Last Theorem states that the equation xⁿ + yⁿ = zⁿ has no positive integer solutions for n &gt; 2, a problem unsolved for roughly 357 years until Andrew Wiles published a proof in 1995. A formal proof is one whose every logical step has been checked by a computer proof assistant; Lean is one such open-source system that represents mathematical statements and proofs as code the machine can verify. Formal verification has long been pursued to guarantee the correctness of both mathematics and critical software, but the extreme length and complexity of Wiles&\#x27;s argument had made full formalization a major open challenge.

## [Pig Kidney Xenotransplantation as Bridge to Human Transplant: First-in-Human Study](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736%2826%2901295-X/fulltext?rss=yes) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A study published in The Lancet by Leonardo Riella and colleagues reports that a genetically engineered porcine kidney supported a living human recipient for 271 days—the longest dialysis-free xenograft function recorded to date—after which the patient received a successful deceased-donor human kidney transplant. This milestone demonstrates that pig kidneys can serve as a viable bridge therapy for patients facing long waits for human organs, potentially saving lives among the many candidates who die or are delisted before a compatible kidney becomes available. The pig kidney was genetically engineered to reduce immune rejection, and importantly no clinically significant allosensitisation or zoonotic infection was observed. However, late graft failure involved thrombotic microangiopathy—microscopic blood clots in small vessels—progressing despite a negative donor-specific crossmatch, suggesting rejection mechanisms beyond conventional antibody-mediated responses contributed to injury.

rss · The Lancet · 最新文章 · Sep 3, 22:30

**Background**: Xenotransplantation refers to transplanting living cells, tissues, or organs from a non-human animal source into a human recipient. Because the human organ donor pool is severely limited, genetically engineered pigs have been proposed as an alternative source of kidneys. Allotransplantation, by contrast, involves transplanting organs between two humans and triggers immune responses primarily against ABO blood group antigens or donor-specific HLA antigens. Thrombotic microangiopathy \(TMA\) is a pathological condition in which small blood vessels become occluded by platelet-rich clots, leading to platelet destruction, hemolytic anemia, and organ damage. Over the past three years, xenotransplantation has progressed rapidly from preclinical animal models to decedent \(brain-dead donor\) studies and now to living human recipients.

## [Can AI Design Circuit Boards Yet? A Practitioner&\#x27;s Reality Check](https://eebench.org/blog/can-ai-design-circuit-boards-yet/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

A practitioner assessment on eebench.org explores whether current AI tools can effectively design printed circuit boards \(PCBs\), featuring real-world experiments with tools like Claude Opus 4.8, Fable, and the KiCAD MCP Server combined with Codex. The article documents mixed results, ranging from near-complete successes requiring only minor blue-wire fixes to subtle but critical mistakes like missed through-holes and undersized pads. PCB design is a multi-billion-dollar industry that underpins virtually all electronics, and automating even parts of it could dramatically accelerate hardware development cycles. The findings reveal that AI is currently useful for specific, well-bounded tasks but still requires significant human oversight for manufacturing-ready output, setting realistic expectations for the near future of AI-assisted hardware engineering. In one test, Claude Opus 4.8 successfully designed a VGA EEPROM programmer using only 74-series logic and GALs for just $6 in fabrication costs, with only one fixable error. Commercial platforms like Quilter claim to reduce 428 hours of professional layout work to 38.5 hours of human cleanup, while newer entrants like Galvano.ai reportedly handle deeper design tasks than earlier commercial systems.

hackernews · Hacker News \(热门\) · Sep 4, 19:48 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49569366)

**Background**: Printed Circuit Boards \(PCBs\) are the physical boards that mechanically support and electrically connect electronic components. Designing them involves two main phases: schematic capture \(defining the circuit logically\) and layout/routing \(placing components and drawing copper traces on the board\). Electronic Design Automation \(EDA\) tools like KiCad, Altium, and Cadence have long assisted engineers with these tasks. Recent AI-powered platforms such as Flux, Quilter, DeepPCB, and Circuit Mind aim to further automate placement, routing, and even schematic generation. DRC \(Design Rule Check\) tools from manufacturers like JLC and PCBWay verify that a board meets fabrication constraints before production.
