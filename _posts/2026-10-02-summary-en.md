## [Rust 1.99.0 Released with Stabilized C-ABI Variadic Functions](https://blog.rust-lang.org/2026/10/01/Rust-1.99.0/) ⭐️ 9.0/10

The Rust team has officially released Rust 1.99.0, announced via the Rust Blog on October 1, 2026. The most notable change is the stabilization of variadic functions using the C and C-unwind ABIs, allowing Rust to interoperate more easily with C libraries expecting variable argument lists. This release is significant for the entire Rust ecosystem, as it removes a long-standing pain point in C interop — previously, Rust developers had to use unsafe workarounds to call variadic C functions like printf. Stabilizing this capability makes Rust more practical for systems programming, embedding into C codebases, and binding to existing native libraries. The variadic function support is restricted to extern C and C-unwind ABIs and is enabled through the existing variadic\_args feature, meaning it does not alter Rust&\#x27;s native ABI. Users with prior Rust installations via rustup can upgrade with \`rustup update stable\`, and the release includes the usual set of compiler, standard library, and Cargo improvements.

rss · Lobsters \(技术社区\) · Oct 1, 13:08

**Background**: Rust is a general-purpose systems programming language that focuses on performance, type safety, concurrency, and memory safety without requiring a garbage collector. It reached version 1.0 in May 2015 and is developed under the stewardship of the Rust Foundation. Variadic functions — functions that accept a variable number of arguments, such as printf in C — were not natively expressible in safe Rust prior to this release, requiring developers to write unsafe FFI shims.

## [Survodutide Phase 3 SYNCHRONIZE-2 Results in Obesity with T2D](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2607219?ai=nejm&amp;af=R&amp;rss=currentIssue) ⭐️ 9.0/10

The New England Journal of Medicine has published the SYNCHRONIZE-2 Phase 3 trial evaluating once-weekly survodutide for weight management in adults with obesity and type 2 diabetes. The 76-week, double-blind, placebo-controlled study randomized 752 participants with T2D and BMI ≥27 to survodutide 3.6 mg, 6.0 mg, or placebo, achieving approximately 13.1% body weight reduction. This represents pivotal evidence for survodutide, the first dual GLP-1/glucagon receptor agonist to reach Phase 3 for metabolic disease, potentially establishing a new treatment paradigm for the large overlapping populations of obesity and T2D. The results position survodutide competitively against existing dual incretin agonists like tirzepatide. Survodutide is a 29-residue peptide built on a glucagon backbone that acts as a dual agonist at both GLP-1 and glucagon receptors, developed by Boehringer Ingelheim in partnership with Zealand Pharma. Earlier Phase 2 data had shown up to 18.7% body weight reduction at the maximum dose over 46 weeks, while the dual mechanism also demonstrated hepatic fat reduction of up to 55% in MASH studies.

rss · NEJM · 最新文章 · Oct 1, 07:30

**Background**: GLP-1 receptor agonists have transformed obesity and T2D treatment by mimicking the incretin hormone glucagon-like peptide-1, which suppresses appetite and improves glycemic control. Dual GLP-1/glucagon agonists aim to combine appetite suppression with increased energy expenditure through glucagon receptor activation, potentially producing greater weight loss than single-mechanism agents. Survodutide enters a competitive landscape that includes tirzepatide \(a dual GIP/GLP-1 agonist\) and emerging GLP-1/glucagon dual agonists.

## [FDA Approves First Heart Valve Designed to Grow with Children](http://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-approves-first-heart-valve-designed-grow-children) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The U.S. FDA has approved the Autus Size-Adjustable Valve, a surgically implanted pulmonary heart valve designed specifically for pediatric patients with congenital pulmonary valve disease. It is the first heart valve engineered to expand as a child grows, eliminating the need for repeated open-heart valve replacements. This approval marks a paradigm shift in pediatric cardiac care, as traditional prosthetic pulmonary valves are fixed in size and force children to undergo multiple invasive replacement surgeries as they outgrow the device. By reducing the number of open-heart operations a child must endure, the device could significantly lower surgical risks, reduce healthcare costs, and improve long-term quality of life for thousands of young patients with congenital heart disease. The valve can be initially implanted at approximately 13 millimeters in diameter—suitable for toddlers or preschool-aged children—and later expanded non-invasively using a balloon catheter as the child grows. The device was invented at Boston Children&\#x27;s Hospital and addresses a long-standing limitation of fixed-size prosthetic valves in growing patients.

rss · FDA Press Releases \(国际\) · Oct 1, 19:02

**Background**: Congenital pulmonary valve disease, including pulmonary stenosis, is a heart defect present at birth in which the pulmonary valve \(located between the right ventricle and the pulmonary artery\) is too small or too narrow, forcing the right ventricle to work harder to pump blood to the lungs. Pulmonary valvular stenosis accounts for approximately 10% of all congenital heart defects \(CHDs\). Traditional treatment involves implanting prosthetic valves that are fixed in size; because children&\#x27;s hearts grow rapidly, these valves must be surgically replaced multiple times throughout childhood, each procedure carrying the risks of open-heart surgery.

Researchers developed an AI system that defeated the best human Stratego player in history by adding a second neural network specifically designed to infer the identity of hidden opponent pieces. This breakthrough marks the first time an AI has mastered Stratego at a championship level. Stratego has long been considered one of the most challenging benchmarks for AI due to its imperfect information nature—unlike chess or Go, players cannot see opponents&\#x27; pieces. Solving this problem demonstrates progress in AI reasoning under uncertainty, with potential applications to real-world domains like negotiations, cybersecurity, and military strategy where information is incomplete. The key technical innovation was a dual neural network architecture: one network for strategic decision-making and a second dedicated network for probabilistic inference about hidden pieces. Notably, the system achieved this on a relatively modest computational budget compared to the massive resources typically required for game-playing AI breakthroughs like AlphaGo.

rss · Ars Technica · Oct 1, 16:28

**Background**: Stratego is a two-player board game similar to Capture the Flag, where each player has 40 pieces of varying ranks and abilities. Unlike chess or Go where all pieces are visible, Stratego&\#x27;s defining feature is that players cannot see their opponent&\#x27;s piece identities, creating a game of imperfect information with elements of memory, deduction, and bluffing. This makes it comparable to poker in terms of strategic complexity. Previous AI systems had struggled with Stratego precisely because they could not reliably infer hidden information, even as AI had conquered perfect-information games like chess \(Deep Blue, 1997\) and Go \(AlphaGo, 2016\).

## [Neoadjuvant Immunotherapy Outperforms Targeted Therapy in Melanoma: 5-Year INMC Data](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04677-z) ⭐️ 8.0/10

An updated pooled analysis from the International Neoadjuvant Melanoma Consortium \(INMC\), published in Nature Medicine on October 1, 2026, reports 3- and 5-year survival outcomes for 1,038 melanoma patients treated with neoadjuvant therapy. The study found that immune checkpoint inhibitors \(ICIs\) alone or in combination were superior to BRAF/MEK inhibitors, and that patients achieving major pathological responses had significantly better long-term survival. This is the largest pooled analysis to date with mature long-term survival data for neoadjuvant therapy in melanoma, directly informing treatment selection between ICIs and BRAF/MEK inhibitors in resectable disease. The findings also reinforce major pathological response as a validated prognostic biomarker, potentially accelerating future clinical trial design by enabling earlier surrogate endpoints. The cohort of 1,038 patients was treated with neoadjuvant ICIs, BRAF/MEK inhibitors, or ICI–targeted therapy combinations, with major pathological response defined as ≥90% tumor cell kill in the surgical specimen serving as a key correlative endpoint. ICIs \(as monotherapy or combinations\) demonstrated superior outcomes compared with BRAF/MEK inhibitors, whose responses—though initially rapid—are known to be less durable, with five-year data previously showing long-term benefit in only about one-third of patients.

rss · Nature Medicine · Oct 1, 00:00

**Background**: Neoadjuvant therapy is induction treatment given before surgery to shrink tumors, improve resectability, and reduce the risk of incomplete resection, in contrast to adjuvant therapy which is given after surgery. In melanoma, two main classes of neoadjuvant agents are used: immune checkpoint inhibitors \(ICIs\), which harness the immune system to attack cancer cells, and BRAF/MEK inhibitors, which target specific genetic mutations \(most commonly BRAF V600E\) driving tumor growth. Major pathological response has been validated in other cancers such as lung cancer as a surrogate endpoint for overall survival, allowing trials to reach conclusions faster than waiting for survival data.
