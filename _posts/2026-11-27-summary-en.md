## [STOPCAP identifies who benefits most from ARPIs in advanced prostate cancer](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736%2826%2901483-2/fulltext?rss=yes) ⭐️ 9.0/10

A STOPCAP systematic review and individual participant data meta-analysis found that adding an androgen receptor pathway inhibitor to standard care significantly improved overall survival for most patients with metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer. Abiraterone improved cancer-specific survival across groups, but non-cancer mortality increased among older patients, highlighting the need to consider age and comorbidities when selecting treatment. The findings strengthen the case for treatment intensification in metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer while showing that treatment should be individualized rather than applied uniformly. Older adults and patients with substantial comorbidities may face a different balance of survival benefits and non-cancer risks, potentially affecting clinical and patient decision-making. The analysis synthesized individual participant data from 13 randomized trials in which androgen deprivation therapy was intensified with an ARPI, with or without docetaxel; these trials had consistently shown improvements in progression-free and overall survival. The authors cautioned that additional individual participant data are needed to fully determine how ARPI effects vary by age, and the review itself does not establish a universally optimal drug choice for every subgroup.

rss · The Lancet · 最新文章 · Nov 26, 23:30

**Background**: Metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer retains responsiveness to androgen deprivation therapy, but the addition of an ARPI has become an important intensification strategy. ARPIs target the androgen receptor signaling pathway, which drives prostate-cancer growth and progression. STOPCAP is a collaboration designed to pool individual participant data from randomized trials so that treatment effects can be assessed more reliably across patient subgroups.

## [Why Mainstream Programming Needs Verifiable Languages](https://blainehansen.me/post/mainstream-verifiable-language/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The article argues for designing a widely adopted, verification-oriented mainstream programming language rather than expecting formal methods to remain specialized extensions of existing languages. It frames AI-assisted development, proofs, and code analysis as potential ways to reduce the steep learning curve associated with formal verification. If verification tools are easier to learn and use, software teams may be able to catch defects and prove important properties before deployment rather than discovering them through testing and incidents. Wider adoption could also connect formal verification with AI-generated code, where stronger guarantees may be especially valuable. Existing systems mentioned in the discussion include SPARK, Dafny, Verus, Frama-C, Why3, TLA+, and Zena, each addressing different languages, proof styles, or execution models. A central caveat is that mainstream status cannot be assumed from technical design: it must come from adoption, developer experience, ecosystem support, and successful migration from established codebases.

hackernews · Hacker News \(热门\) · Nov 27, 05:22 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50663089)

**Background**: Formal verification uses mathematical reasoning to demonstrate that programs satisfy specified properties, with Hoare logic providing a classical framework for reasoning about correctness. Some verification-oriented languages and type systems extend this approach with dependent types, refinement types, effects, or built-in specifications; Dafny, for example, combines specification constructs with a static verifier for functional correctness. Proof assistants such as Lean and Isabelle offer powerful foundations for checked proofs, while research into AI-assisted autoformalization explores whether large language models can help formulate and prove theorems without compromising kernel-based assurance.
