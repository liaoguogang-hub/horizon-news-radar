## [Critical Linux IPv4 TCP Use-After-Free Flaw Disclosed](https://atlasum.net/blog/cve-2026-98432-linux-ipv4-tcp-uaf) ⭐️ 9.0/10

CVE-2026-98432 was disclosed as a critical Linux kernel IPv4 TCP use-after-free vulnerability with a reported CVSS score of 9.8. The flaw affects the TCP connection-request processing path and may allow a remote unauthenticated attacker to cause memory corruption, code execution, or a crash under specific conditions. Linux kernels 4.4 and later were reported to be affected, placing a large share of internet-facing servers and cloud systems at potential risk. The lack of timely patches in major distributions such as Ubuntu and Red Hat Enterprise Linux increases operational concern, even though practical internet exploitation may be constrained by packet-filtering and processing conditions. The issue is a race-related use-after-free during IPv4 TCP handshake processing, including SYNACK processing, rather than an MPTCP-specific defect; crafted IPv4 packets with options and certain NetLabel/CIPSO configurations may also trigger it. Community analysis cited Red Hat guidance that dropping IPv4 packets with options can reduce exposure but is not a complete mitigation, while exploitation reportedly requires multi-core packet processing, making single-core or single-CPU-pinned systems less affected.

hackernews · Hacker News \(热门\) · Oct 31, 19:16 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50319143)

**Background**: A use-after-free occurs when code continues to access memory after the corresponding allocation has been released, potentially causing invalid reads, memory corruption, or crashes. In this case, the vulnerable code is in the Linux kernel&\#x27;s IPv4 networking subsystem, so exploitation through crafted network traffic could cross a major security boundary without requiring application-level authentication. TCP handshake processing is an early phase in establishing a connection, making the affected listener role and kernel networking configuration important to exposure.

## [x86-64 Performance Counters Show Non-Determinism and Overcounting](https://web.eece.maine.edu/~vweaver/projects/deterministic/deterministic_counters.pdf) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A study of 11 x86\_64 CPU implementations found that nominally exact events, including retired instructions, can vary across runs and overcount even in tightly controlled environments. Only a small subset of events, such as conditional branches and retired stores, proved sufficiently deterministic without adjustment. These hardware-counter behaviors undermine systems that use instruction counts to coordinate threads or reproduce executions, including deterministic replay and deterministic threading libraries. They also show why conventional profilers and logical-clock designs cannot assume that every PMU event is an exact, repeatable metric.
