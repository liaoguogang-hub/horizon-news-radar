## [Burau Representation of Braid Group B₄ Proven Faithful](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05283) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A new paper proves that the Burau representation of the braid group B₄ is faithful, meaning that different braids always yield different matrices under this classical representation. This resolves a long-standing open case for the specific value n=4 of a conjecture dating back to the 1930s. This result is significant for pure mathematics because it fills a critical gap in understanding the Burau representation across different values of n. It has direct implications for knot theory and low-dimensional topology, where braid group representations underpin many invariants such as Alexander polynomials and connections to quantum invariants like the Jones polynomial. The faithfulness of the Burau representation was previously known only for small cases such as n=2, 3, and 5, and was disproven for n≥5 entirely by Bigelow and Krammer in the early 2000s. Thus, the n=4 case was the last remaining open value of n for which faithfulness could possibly hold, making this proof especially noteworthy.

rss · Hacker News \(热门\) · Jul 27, 23:46

**Background**: The braid group Bₙ is a fundamental algebraic object in topology, whose elements can be visualized as braids of n strands and which connects closely to knot theory via the closure operation. A representation of a group is a way to map its elements to matrices in a way that respects the group structure; such a representation is faithful if this map is injective, meaning distinct group elements always produce distinct matrices. The Burau representation, introduced in the 1930s, is one of the most classical matrix representations of the braid groups and plays a central role in the theory of the Alexander polynomial and related topological invariants.

It targeted the Lorenz SZ-40/42 cipher used by the German High Command, a more complex system than the well-known Enigma, employing a clock pulse to synchronize processing operations—an innovation that distinguished it as a precursor to modern computer architecture.

**Background**: During World War II, British codebreakers at Bletchley Park worked to decrypt two types of German encrypted transmissions: Enigma messages sent in Morse code, and the lesser-known &\#x27;Fish&\#x27; transmissions sent via teleprinter starting in 1941. The Lorenz cipher \(codenamed &\#x27;Tunny&\#x27; by the British\) was used by the German High Command and operated by mixing plaintext teleprinter code with pseudo-random key letters generated by 12 internal wheels. Mathematician Max Newman led the effort to mechanize the breaking of Lorenz messages, establishing the &\#x27;Newmanry&\#x27; unit, while his predecessor machine &\#x27;Heath Robinson&\#x27; proved too slow, motivating Flowers to build the fully electronic Colossus. Ten Colossus machines were eventually delivered, and the entire program remained secret until the 1970s.
