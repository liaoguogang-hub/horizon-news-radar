## [AI-Assisted Zero-Click Worm Spreads via WeChat Calls on iOS and Android](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/10/calif-research/) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Calif Research publicly demonstrated WeWorm, the first zero-click worm that spreads through WeChat voice calls across both iOS and Android, allowing attackers to hijack accounts without any user interaction. Using AI assistance, the team found the underlying bug and wrote the initial remote code execution exploit in about two days, with the full worm built in roughly one more week. WeChat has over a billion users, making this exploit class a massive potential threat to mobile security and personal privacy, especially given that no user action is required for compromise. The dramatic compression of development time—from months for a traditional team to days with AI—signals a meaningful escalation in AI-augmented offensive cyber capabilities and raises urgent questions for the AI safety community about the dual-use risks of generative AI in security research. Victims do not need to answer the incoming call, and even if they do, they hear nothing while the exploit succeeds silently in the background. The worm leverages a vulnerability in WeChat&\#x27;s custom browser component \(related to the XWalk runtime previously documented by Talos\), and Tencent has reportedly already blocked the exploit after private disclosure. Calif emphasized that humans provided the strategic judgment about targeting and safe testing, while AI handled most of the technical exploitation work.

rss · Simon Willison \(AI 跨行业洞察\) · Sep 10, 00:56

**Background**: A zero-click exploit is one that requires no interaction from the victim—no taps, links, or answers—making it especially dangerous because there is nothing a user can do to avoid it. A worm is malware that self-propagates, automatically spreading from one compromised device to others \(in this case, the victim&\#x27;s WeChat contacts\). Remote code execution \(RCE\) means the attacker can run arbitrary code on the victim&\#x27;s device, potentially reading messages, making calls, or chaining further attacks. WeChat, operated by Tencent, is one of the world&\#x27;s largest messaging platforms with particularly deep penetration in China and across Asia, which amplifies the impact of any vulnerability affecting it. AI-assisted exploit development refers to the emerging use of large language models to accelerate vulnerability discovery, reverse engineering, and exploit writing—tasks that previously demanded specialized human expertise over extended timelines.

## [CRISP: Foundation Model for Intraoperative Frozen Section Pathology](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04703-0) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Published in Nature Medicine, CRISP is a vision-based pathology foundation model trained exclusively on frozen section slides, outperforming existing foundation models and supporting intraoperative treatment decisions with prospective clinical validation. Intraoperative pathology directly guides surgical decisions while the patient is still on the operating table, and a foundation model purpose-built for frozen section slides could reduce diagnostic turnaround time, improve accuracy on these morphologically challenging specimens, and ultimately expand the scope and safety of AI-assisted surgery. Unlike most pathology foundation models \(such as Atlas from Mayo Clinic, Charité, and Aignostics\) that are trained on diverse whole-slide images, CRISP is trained specifically on frozen section data, which is known for lower morphological quality due to the rapid freezing process.

rss · Nature Medicine · Sep 10, 00:00

**Background**: Foundation models in pathology are large neural networks, typically based on Vision Transformers, that are pre-trained on massive histopathology image collections using self-supervised or weakly supervised learning and then adapted to downstream diagnostic tasks. Frozen section slides are prepared intraoperatively by rapidly freezing tissue so that a pathologist can examine it within minutes, guiding the surgeon&\#x27;s next steps; however, because of the speed of preparation, these slides tend to have poorer morphological quality than standard formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded slides, making automated analysis more difficult. CRISP addresses this gap by being purpose-built for the unique visual characteristics of frozen sections rather than reusing models trained on higher-quality permanent sections.

## [LLMs Extract Clinical Data from EHRs into Patient Journey Knowledge Graphs](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04695-x) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Published in Nature Medicine on September 10, 2026, this study presents a suite of large pre-trained language models that accurately extract computable clinical data from unstructured electronic health records \(EHRs\) and integrate the results into knowledge graphs capable of modeling patient trajectories and treatment responses in real-world settings. This work bridges a critical gap in healthcare AI by making the vast amounts of unstructured clinical text—such as symptom narratives, adverse effects, and treatment rationales—computationally accessible at scale. By enabling longitudinal patient journey modeling, it has meaningful implications for real-world evidence generation, clinical research, treatment optimization, and population health analytics. The approach combines a suite of specialized clinical language models with knowledge graph construction techniques, allowing both structured and unstructured EHR data to be unified into a single computable representation of patient trajectories. The methodology focuses on real-world applicability, addressing the challenge that most clinically rich information resides in free-text encounter notes rather than coded fields.

rss · Nature Medicine · Sep 10, 00:00

**Background**: Electronic health records contain a mix of structured data \(e.g., lab values, diagnostic codes\) and unstructured data \(e.g., physician notes, discharge summaries\). The unstructured portion holds the majority of clinically meaningful detail—such as symptom narratives, adverse effects, treatment rationales, and adherence challenges—but has historically been difficult to use at scale. Large pre-trained language models, particularly clinical transformer-based LLMs like GatorTron, have recently enabled much more accurate extraction of information from clinical text. Knowledge graphs provide a structured way to represent relationships between biomedical concepts, patients, and events, and integrating LLM-extracted findings into such graphs enables longitudinal analysis of patient journeys over time.

## [Spanda: Sub-Microsecond LLM Uncertainty Engine in Rust](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49663329) ⭐️ 7.0/10

The open-source project Spanda, written in Rust, performs epistemic uncertainty quantification and hallucination gating for LLMs in under one microsecond on CPU \(760 ns\). It introduces exact-match normalized entropy \(R\_sc\), which achieves the same discriminative AUROC as Semantic Entropy on the GSM8K reasoning benchmark while eliminating the 90 ms NLI cross-encoder \(DeBERTa\) bottleneck. Hallucination detection has been a major blocker for deploying LLMs in production, because the standard Semantic Entropy method requires a slow NLI model that adds tens to hundreds of milliseconds per request and requires a GPU. By replacing that with a pure-CPU exact-match entropy computation that is roughly 20,000× faster and uses ~75× less memory, Spanda makes real-time uncertainty gating feasible at the gateway layer for any production LLM service. Benchmarked against a Python proxy, Spanda measures 760 ns kernel latency vs 15 ms, ~3 MB RSS vs 230 MB, and 3.6 ms cold start vs 1.17 s. The authors note mode-collapse behavior on 70B+ models as an open question, meaning R\_sc may be less informative when outputs converge to identical strings despite semantic disagreement.

rss · Hacker News \(best\) · Sep 11, 18:48

**Background**: LLMs sometimes produce confident-sounding but factually wrong outputs, known as hallucinations. Kuhn et al. \(Nature, 2024\) proposed Semantic Entropy, which samples multiple answers, groups them by meaning using a Natural Language Inference \(NLI\) cross-encoder like DeBERTa-v3-large, and computes entropy over those semantic clusters—high entropy signals likely hallucination. Because every pair of candidate answers must be scored by the NLI model, the cost grows quadratically with sample count and typically requires a GPU, making the method impractical for high-throughput or latency-sensitive deployments. Spanda challenges this by showing that, for structured reasoning tasks where answers are short and discrete \(e.g., math word problems in GSM8K\), a simple exact-match entropy over output strings is just as discriminative.
