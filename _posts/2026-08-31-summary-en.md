## [Sham-Controlled RCT: AF Catheter Ablation Shows No Quality-of-Life Benefit](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736%2826%2901558-8/fulltext?rss=yes) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The PVI-SHAM-AF trial, a randomized, double-blind, sham-controlled, multicentre study published in The Lancet, found that catheter ablation did not outperform a sham procedure in improving atrial fibrillation-related quality of life at 6 months. This rigorously designed trial directly challenges the assumed efficacy of a widely adopted interventional treatment. Catheter ablation has become a standard treatment for symptomatic atrial fibrillation, performed millions of times worldwide, so a sham-controlled negative result has major implications for clinical guidelines, reimbursement decisions, and patient informed consent. It also highlights how the placebo effect can inflate perceived benefits of invasive procedures when only open-label comparisons are used. The sham-controlled design — in which patients in the control arm undergo a simulated procedure without actual ablation — is the gold-standard method for isolating true treatment effect from placebo and procedural ritual, but is rarely used for device/procedural trials due to ethical and logistical complexity. The primary endpoint was AF-specific quality of life at 6 months, and the absence of superiority suggests earlier open-label trials may have overestimated the procedure&\#x27;s benefit.

rss · The Lancet · 最新文章 · Aug 30, 14:30

**Background**: Atrial fibrillation \(AF\) is the most common cardiac arrhythmia, affecting roughly 33 million people globally, and is associated with stroke, heart failure, and reduced quality of life. Catheter ablation, most commonly via pulmonary vein isolation \(PVI\), uses heat or cold energy to create scar tissue that blocks the abnormal electrical signals triggering AF, and is recommended for patients whose symptoms are not adequately controlled by medication. Sham-controlled trials, in which a placebo procedure mimics the real intervention, are rarely conducted in interventional cardiology because of ethical concerns about performing invasive sham procedures, making this trial methodologically notable.

## [Pooled Analysis: β-Blocker Discontinuation Non-Inferior in Stable Post-MI Patients](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736%2826%2901426-1/fulltext?rss=yes) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A pooled individual patient data analysis of the ABYSS and SMART-DECISION trials, published in The Lancet, found that discontinuing β-blockers in stable patients with prior myocardial infarction, preserved left ventricular ejection fraction \(LVEF ≥40%\), and no heart failure met non-inferiority criteria for the primary and key secondary endpoints. If validated, these findings could shift cardiology practice guidelines on long-term β-blocker use for a very large population of post-MI survivors who are stable and free of heart failure, potentially reducing medication burden, side effects, and healthcare costs. The pooled evidence helps reconcile the previously divergent signals from the two parent trials. The primary endpoint was predominantly driven by hospitalisation outcomes and had a composite composition that varied between trials, limiting straightforward interpretation. ABYSS alone \(N=3,698\) had previously failed to demonstrate non-inferiority and showed numerically more cardiovascular hospitalisations with discontinuation, while SMART-DECISION suggested a nominally significant stroke reduction that requires cautious interpretation given the small number of events and lack of multiplicity adjustment.

rss · The Lancet · 最新文章 · Aug 30, 07:30

**Background**: β-blockers have long been a cornerstone of post-myocardial infarction therapy, traditionally prescribed indefinitely based on older trials conducted before widespread use of reperfusion therapy and modern secondary prevention. In recent years, questions have arisen about whether patients who are stable, have preserved left ventricular ejection fraction, and no heart failure still benefit from long-term β-blocker therapy. Non-inferiority trials are designed to show that a new strategy \(here, discontinuation\) is not unacceptably worse than the standard \(continuation\), within a pre-specified margin of difference.

## [Kale: A Transformation-Safe Spreadsheet System](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26345) ⭐️ 7.0/10

A new research paper introduces Kale, a spreadsheet system designed to safely handle structural transformations. When a user changes the structure of a referenced table, Kale ensures that range references are updated correctly to point to the new range, preventing formula errors during modifications. Spreadsheet errors caused by structural changes are a common and costly problem in business, science, and engineering, where spreadsheets remain one of the most widely used end-user programming tools. Kale&\#x27;s formal approach to transformation safety addresses a long-standing reliability gap and could influence how future spreadsheet systems handle complex dependencies. The paper specifically tackles the problem that spreadsheet formulas can refer to rectangular ranges of arbitrary size, and focuses on ensuring correct behavior when those ranges shift due to structural edits. This positions Kale at the intersection of programming language theory and human-computer interaction research.

rss · Lobsters \(技术社区\) · Aug 31, 18:32

**Background**: Spreadsheets are one of the most successful end-user programming environments, used by hundreds of millions of people for data analysis, financial modeling, and scientific computation. A key feature is the ability for formulas to reference rectangular ranges of cells, but when the underlying table structure changes—such as inserting or deleting rows or columns—the system must update these references. Traditional spreadsheet engines handle this automatically but can produce silent errors, misaligned references, or unexpected results, which is a well-documented source of costly mistakes. Transformation-safe systems aim to apply formal guarantees, similar to those in programming language research, to ensure correctness during such modifications.
