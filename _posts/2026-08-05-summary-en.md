A position paper titled &quot;LLMs Can&\#x27;t Jump&quot; by Tom Zahavy \(DeepMind\) argues that large language models are fundamentally incapable of making the creative leaps required for scientific discovery, because human experience encoded in language is inherently lossy. The paper has sparked significant discussion on OpenReview with 216 points and 149 comments. This challenges a widely-held assumption in AI research and industry that scaling LLMs will eventually lead to AI systems capable of making novel scientific discoveries. The debate has implications for how the research community frames the role of LLMs in science and whether current approaches can ever reach human-level creative reasoning. The author later clarified on social media that the paper should not be interpreted as claiming LLMs can never make real discoveries, and noted that mischaracterizations such as &quot;DeepMind is throwing cold water on AI for science&quot; misrepresent the paper&\#x27;s nuance. The paper frames language itself as a lossy encoding of human experience, meaning that experiential knowledge crucial for intuitive leaps is inevitably stripped away during textual representation.

The concept of language as a &quot;lossy&quot; encoding draws from information theory: lossy compression permanently discards some information to reduce size, analogous to how words cannot fully capture subjective, embodied experience. The study connects two previously separate lines of research — sycophancy in LLM outputs and psychological measures of prosociality — and shows experimentally that exposure to agreeable, validating AI responses shifts user attitudes and self-reported willingness to help others. ## [Memristors Achieve Thousands of Conductance Levels on CMOS](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-05759-5) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A Nature paper demonstrates 256×256 memristor arrays monolithically integrated on commercial CMOS circuits, achieving 2,048 distinguishable conductance levels in individual memristors. This level of programmable precision was realized in a foundry fabrication process rather than only in laboratory devices. High-precision, multi-level conductance is essential for using memristors as analog synaptic weights in neural network hardware, enabling accurate and uniform performance across large memristive networks. By achieving this on a commercial CMOS process, the work significantly advances the practical feasibility of in-memory computing and neuromorphic systems that could surpass traditional von Neumann architectures in energy efficiency. The arrays contain 256×256 memristors fabricated in a commercial foundry via monolithic integration on CMOS, with each device supporting 2,048 conductance levels—orders of magnitude more than typical binary memory cells. The emphasis on factory-fabricated devices \(not just laboratory prototypes\) addresses a key gap between research demos and deployable neuromorphic hardware.

rss · Hacker News \(best\) · Aug 5, 20:35

**Background**: A memristor is a non-volatile memory element whose resistance \(or conductance\) depends on the history of charge that has flowed through it, making it useful for storing analog values rather than just binary 0s and 1s. In-memory computing \(IMC\) and neuromorphic computing seek to perform computation directly within memory arrays, eliminating the data-movement bottleneck of conventional computers where memory and processor are separated. Both paradigms rely on analog memory devices with many precise, tunable conductance levels to represent synaptic weights in neural networks; until now, achieving thousands of such levels on a manufacturing-compatible CMOS process has been a major obstacle.

## [Keyv and Cacheable npm Packages Hit by Supply Chain Attack](https://dev.to/informertech/keyv-and-cacheable-npm-supply-chain-compromise-4n6e) ⭐️ 8.0/10

On August 4, 2026, at least ten npm packages in the keyv and cacheable namespaces were published with a malicious \`setup.mjs\` preinstall hook that downloads a standalone Bun runtime, executes obfuscated second-stage payloads, and exfiltrates cloud, CI, GitHub, npm, and Kubernetes credentials. The compromised \`Jaredwray\` maintainer account allowed the attacker to use stolen npm tokens to spread the trojanized hook into packages outside the keyv/cacheable namespaces, including \`@thiennq/docs-viewer@1.6.2\`. Because keyv, flat-cache, file-entry-cache, and cacheable sit deep in dependency trees of tools like eslint, tens of millions of weekly downloads and far more end-user installs were exposed to credential-stealing malware. The worm-like lateral spread via stolen npm tokens \(coined &\#x27;Shai-Hulud&\#x27; by researchers\) can republish trojanized versions of any package the compromised maintainer can reach, turning a single account takeover into an ecosystem-wide incident. The malicious \`package.json\` runs a \`preinstall\` script invoking \`setup.mjs\`, which uses \`findBunExecutable\` to locate a bundled Bun runtime and then runs \`bun \_environment.js\`; stolen data is encrypted and sent to GitHub repositories and DNS-resolved hosts, while \`.claude\` and \`.vscode\` directories in cloned repos get autostart persistence hooks. Socket&\#x27;s scanner flagged the affected tarballs an average of five minutes and 18 seconds after publication, and unscoped \`@keyv/\*\` 6.0.0 packages published just before \`keyv@6.0.0\` lack the confirmed preinstall hook but should still be treated as suspect.

rss · Dev.to · Aug 5, 20:00

**Background**: \`keyv\` is a simple key-value storage abstraction for Node.js with adapters for Redis, SQLite, Postgres, MongoDB, and other backends, and it is widely used as a caching layer. The related \`cacheable\`, \`cacheable-request\`, \`flat-cache\`, and \`file-entry-cache\` packages are caching utilities maintained within the same ecosystem. Because they are common transitive dependencies — for example \`eslint\` → \`file-entry-cache\` → \`flat-cache\` → \`keyv\` — many projects install them without listing them directly. An npm \`preinstall\` hook is a script defined in \`package.json\` that npm automatically runs before the actual package code is installed, making it a powerful vector for executing attacker code on developer and CI machines.
