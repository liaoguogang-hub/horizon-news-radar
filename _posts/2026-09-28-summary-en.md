## [Google AI Overviews Confidently Hallucinating False Information](https://sancho.bearblog.dev/google-weird/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

A widely-discussed blog post critiques Google Search&\#x27;s AI Overviews for confidently presenting fabricated or hallucinated information to users, with concrete examples including a query about &\#x27;Dario staying in Turkey&\#x27; memes that triggered AI-generated nonsense, and a sports query where Google falsely claimed the Halifax Wanderers had already secured a playoff position when they had not. This matters because Google&\#x27;s AI Overviews appear at the very top of search results for hundreds of millions of users, meaning fabricated information from hallucinations is being presented with Google&\#x27;s institutional authority and high visibility. The erosion of trust in a previously reliable information gateway has broad implications for public knowledge, journalism, and how people verify facts online. The hallucinated content is particularly dangerous because it is presented in a declarative, authoritative tone rather than with uncertainty disclaimers, and the underlying issue stems from well-known limitations of large language models \(LLMs\), which can produce plausible-sounding but entirely fabricated outputs \(fabricated citations, invented facts, and non-existent entities\).

hackernews · Hacker News \(热门\) · Sep 27, 20:12 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49870367)

**Background**: Google AI Overviews is a generative AI feature integrated into Google Search that produces AI-generated summaries at the top of search results, launched to give users quick and structured insights. The technology is built on large language models \(LLMs\), which are neural networks trained on massive text corpora to predict and generate natural language. A well-known weakness of LLMs is &\#x27;hallucination,&\#x27; where the model produces output that sounds confident and plausible but is not grounded in factual reality. Because LLMs generate text by predicting probable word sequences rather than by retrieving verified facts, they can invent citations, statistics, or events that never occurred, and this problem becomes especially harmful when the output is presented as authoritative search results.

## [ESP32S3 Cluster Runs 1.58-bit BitNet Language Model](https://github.com/Low-Zi-Hong/ESP32s3-LLM-Cluster) ⭐️ 7.0/10

A GitHub project demonstrates a cluster of ESP32S3 microcontrollers successfully running a 1.58-bit BitNet language model, showcasing extreme quantization on extremely resource-constrained hardware. The project highlights an innovative approach to distributing LLM inference across multiple low-cost microcontroller units. This project pushes the boundary of what&\#x27;s possible for running large language models on edge devices, potentially enabling local AI inference on inexpensive, ubiquitous hardware without cloud connectivity. It could have implications for IoT applications, privacy-sensitive deployments, and ultra-low-power AI scenarios where cost and energy are critical constraints. The ESP32S3 features a dual-core Xtensa LX7 processor with integrated Wi-Fi and Bluetooth 5, typically priced around $10-$15 per board. BitNet&\#x27;s ternary quantization \(-1, 0, 1\) reduces memory footprint to approximately 1.58 bits per parameter, making it feasible to run on hardware with very limited RAM, though inference speed and practical utility on such constrained devices remain limited.

rss · Hacker News \(热门\) · Sep 28, 21:26

**Background**: BitNet is an architecture introduced by Microsoft Research that uses ternary quantization, representing each parameter with only three values: -1, 0, and 1. This results in a model that uses just 1.58 bits per parameter, significantly reducing computational and memory requirements compared to full-precision models. The ESP32S3 is a low-cost System-on-Chip microcontroller from Espressif, featuring a dual-core Xtensa LX7 processor, Wi-Fi, and Bluetooth 5 connectivity, commonly used in IoT and embedded systems projects. Combining extreme quantization with microcontroller clusters represents an experimental exploration of running AI workloads on the most minimal hardware possible.
