## [Astra Edits Qwen Weights to Improve Reasoning](https://rikiyat.github.io/blog/weight-space-programming/) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Astra identified a weight direction associated with looping in small Qwen reasoning models and edited 131,072 weights across layers 15 and 19. The reported edit raised Qwen3.5-0.8B&\#x27;s MATH-500 score from 7.9% to 48.1% by removing the looping behavior. This suggests that an AI system can inspect, diagnose, and directly repair another model&\#x27;s parameters rather than relying only on prompts, fine-tuning, or inference-time steering. If independently replicated, the approach could become a practical method for targeted model repair and mechanistic interpretability. The edited direction was subtracted from 64 output columns in the MLPs of layers 15 and 19, amounting to 0.016% of the model&\#x27;s nominal 800 million parameters; replacement values were calculated with Python. The base model&\#x27;s first 96 practice answers included 85 that reached the 32,768-token limit, and some visual-answer evaluations did not align with the claims, leaving rigorous replication and broader evaluation necessary.

hackernews · Hacker News \(热门\) · Nov 2, 18:21 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50341174)

**Background**: Weight-space programming means using an AI system to inspect a neural network&\#x27;s numerical parameters, infer which components produce a behavior, and edit those weights directly. It differs from activation steering, which temporarily changes a model&\#x27;s internal activations during inference without permanently modifying its parameters. The reported experiment focuses on reasoning loops, a failure mode in which small models repeatedly generate text until they exhaust the context window instead of producing an answer.

Researchers at SLAC report experimental evidence establishing altermagnetism as a distinct magnetic phase. The result turns a long-proposed form of unconventional magnetism into an experimentally demonstrated phase and opens potential routes for advanced electronics. Altermagnetic materials combine spin splitting with zero net magnetization, a combination that could be useful for spintronics, quantum technologies, memory devices, and other systems requiring spin-controlled electronic behavior. Establishing the phase also provides a foundation for screening and engineering new quantum materials. Altermagnetism is associated with unconventional collinear compensated magnetic order, often described as having d-wave or higher even-parity character, and can produce symmetry-protected spin splitting without a net magnetization. **Background**: In conventional antiferromagnets, opposing magnetic moments cancel, but their spin-split electronic bands generally do not share the distinctive symmetry patterns associated with altermagnetism. Altermagnetism is distinguished by unconventional collinear compensated spin ordering and by crystal-symmetry-driven spin splitting of quasiparticles. The broader claim that it constitutes a separate magnetic phase remains a subject of precise theoretical definition, alongside the reported experimental evidence.
