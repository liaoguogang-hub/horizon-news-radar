## [Clay Mathematics Institute Acknowledges Apparent Navier-Stokes Solution](https://www.claymath.org/news/navier-stokes-announcement/) ⭐️ 9.0/10

The Clay Mathematics Institute \(CMI\) announced that the Navier-Stokes Millennium Prize Problem appears to have been settled, initiating a formal review period. The announcement, notably neutral in tone, does not name OpenAI or any specific solver, instead awaiting formal publication and community verification before any prize decision. The Navier-Stokes problem is one of seven Millennium Prize Problems, each carrying a $1 million reward and representing some of the deepest challenges in mathematics. A verified solution would resolve whether smooth solutions always exist for the equations governing fluid motion—a question with profound implications for physics, engineering, and our understanding of turbulence. CMI&\#x27;s rules require at least two years after publication in a qualifying outlet before any solution can be accepted, giving the mathematical community time to review new results. The proof reportedly includes a Lean 4 formal verification, and CMI&\#x27;s statement notably omits any reference to OpenAI, likely reflecting an ongoing credit dispute and an open letter from Fields Medalists.

hackernews · Hacker News \(热门\) · Sep 12, 04:09 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49668706)

**Background**: The Navier-Stokes equations are a set of partial differential equations that describe the motion of viscous fluid substances and are fundamental to fields ranging from aerodynamics to weather prediction. The specific Millennium Prize problem asks whether smooth, globally defined solutions always exist for these equations in three dimensions—a question that remains unresolved despite the equations&\#x27; widespread practical use. The Clay Mathematics Institute, based in Cambridge, Massachusetts, established the seven Millennium Prize Problems in 2000 to highlight some of the most difficult questions in mathematics.

Apple&\#x27;s ANE is the most opaque programmable engine on Apple Silicon, with no public instruction set, no driver interface, and no documented way to confirm computation actually runs on it. The ANE has since shipped across iPhones, iPads, and M-series Macs, providing hardware acceleration for machine learning inference through Apple&\#x27;s Core ML framework. Unlike CPUs and GPUs, the ANE has remained a black box to outside developers because Apple has never published an official instruction set, programming model, or driver interface, making independent analysis extremely difficult.

## [Reverse Engineering the FSCALE Microcode in Intel&\#x27;s 8087 FPU](https://www.righto.com/2026/09/8087-microcode-reverse-engineering-fscale.html) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Ken Shirriff and the Opcode Collective have published a detailed reverse-engineering analysis of the microcode that implements the FSCALE \(floating-point scale\) instruction inside the Intel 8087 coprocessor. The work reveals how this 1980-era chip executed the instruction at the microcode level. The 8087 established the floating-point standard that most computers still use today, making its internal architecture historically and educationally significant. Detailed microcode analysis of historic processors like the 8087 is rare and provides valuable insights into early computer engineering design decisions. The 8087 implements its floating-point instructions—including transcendental functions like square roots, tangents, and exponentials—using complex low-level microcode rather than purely hardwired logic. The FSCALE instruction is one of many operations in the 8087&\#x27;s instruction set, which also includes FSQRT and FXTRACT, all distinguished by the &\#x27;F&\#x27; prefix indicating floating-point operations.

rss · Hacker News \(热门\) · Sep 12, 15:49

**Background**: The Intel 8087 was a floating-point coprocessor released in 1980, designed to pair with the 8086 CPU and provide hardware-accelerated floating-point arithmetic at approximately 50 KFLOPS. Unlike modern processors that may execute instructions directly in hardware, the 8087 used microcode—a lower-level instruction set stored on-chip—to implement complex mathematical operations including transcendental functions. Reverse engineering this microcode requires physically examining the chip&\#x27;s silicon and painstakingly decoding its internal logic, work typically undertaken by dedicated hardware enthusiasts and historians.

## [In-Place Key Rotation Silently Invalidates Historical Signatures](https://dev.to/mahirhir/both-verifiers-were-honest-agreed-with-each-other-and-the-signatures-still-failed-503n) ⭐️ 6.0/10

A debugging post details how 40 previously valid signatures all failed verification after a key row&\#x27;s \`pub\` and \`scheme\` fields were overwritten in place under the same key ID. Two independent verifiers, both implementing the correct \`sha256\(public\_key \|\| header\)\` recipe, independently reported 0/40 verification, while the retired key material would have verified all 40 records. This case shows that append-only key records must never be edited in place: doing so makes correctly-signed records indistinguishable from forgeries, with no field in the data to tell them apart. It also exposes a sharp limitation of the &\#x27;use two independent verifiers&\#x27; defense — two readers can only detect disagreement between readers, not a blind spot they share. The fix shown in the post is to retain both key rows under the same \`id\` in the key table — one active, one marked \`retired = true\` — so the old material stays available as data for historical verification but is never reused to sign new content. The author emphasizes the operational rule: an in-place edit of a historical row makes everything derived from it indistinguishable from forgery, not merely harder to verify.

**Background**: Digital signatures let a verifier check that a record was produced by the holder of a specific private key, using the corresponding public key and a signature scheme \(such as \`ed25519\` or a keyed hash construction\). In systems that persist signatures over time, key material is often rotated for security hygiene — but rotation must be done by superseding the old key row, never by mutating it. The construction mentioned in the post, \`sha256\(public\_key \|\| header\)\`, is a simple keyed-hash authenticator that binds a header to a public key, analogous in spirit to how hash-based signatures \(e.g., Lamport or Merkle-tree schemes\) tie a signature to a specific key.
