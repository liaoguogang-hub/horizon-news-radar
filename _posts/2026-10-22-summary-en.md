## [eMudhra Exits Global SSL/TLS Certificate Business](https://emudhra.com/en/press-release/emudhra-to-exit-global-ssl-tls-certificate-business) ⭐️ 9.0/10

eMudhra announced that it will exit the global SSL/TLS certificate business after a major certificate-authority incident undermined trust in its issuance infrastructure. The incident involved a failed key-signing ceremony that reportedly enabled the creation of certificates for arbitrary websites. Certificate authorities sit at the foundation of browser trust, so failures in their issuance controls can compromise the authenticity of websites and HTTPS connections. eMudhra&\#x27;s exit affects certificate customers, relying organizations, and the broader public-key infrastructure ecosystem. Community discussion points to use of a four-character RSA private-key value and characterizes the failed ceremony as allowing certificates for any site to be minted. eMudhra claimed that no end-entity certificates were issued from the affected certificate authorities, but commenters challenged that statement because anyone with access could potentially issue such certificates.

**Background**: An SSL/TLS certificate binds a website&\#x27;s identity to a cryptographic key, allowing browsers to verify secure connections. Certificate authorities issue these credentials, and browsers trust certificates through roots contained in their trust stores. A private-key compromise or signing-process failure at a certificate authority can therefore undermine the browser&\#x27;s ability to authenticate websites correctly.

## [eMudhra Intermediate CA Keys Compromised](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/20/emudhra-certificates/) ⭐️ 9.0/10

The private keys of eight eMudhra intermediate certificate authorities were compromised, creating risks of fraudulent certificate issuance under roots trusted by Mozilla, Microsoft, Apple, Chrome, and many downstream trust stores. Software with bundled certificate lists must be updated promptly, including Linux ca-certificates packages and the Python ecosystem. A compromised intermediate CA key can enable attackers to issue fraudulent certificates that may be accepted as valid, potentially enabling TLS man-in-the-middle attacks or abuse of code-signing trust. The incident therefore requires coordinated distrust and certificate-list updates across browsers, operating systems, Linux distributions, package managers, and other trust ecosystems. Six compromised intermediates were associated with TLS and two with code signing, according to the security analysis cited by the news context. Python-related releases include uv 0.13.4, pip 26.2.2, and certifi 2026.10.19, which were released with upgraded certificate lists.

rss · Simon Willison \(AI 跨行业洞察\) · Oct 20, 23:50

**Background**: Root CAs serve as trust anchors in a public key infrastructure, while intermediate CAs issue end-entity certificates under a root&\#x27;s authority. Clients validate certificates by following this chain to a root they trust. Root certificate programs operated by organizations such as Mozilla, Microsoft, Apple, and Chrome determine which root certificates are included in widely used software and trust stores.

Ipilimumab blocks CTLA-4, whereas nivolumab blocks PD-1; combining these mechanisms can strengthen antitumor T-cell activity but may increase toxicity.

FoxIO introduced “Header Accordions,” a QPACK amplification technique that can impose disproportionate memory or bandwidth costs on HTTP/3 servers. One variant inserts an approximately 4 KiB entry into QPACK’s dynamic table and then references it thousands of times with 1-byte indexed references. Because HTTP/3 depends on QPACK to compress request headers, compression can become an attack multiplier rather than merely an efficiency feature. Vulnerable servers may therefore face memory exhaustion or amplified denial-of-service pressure from relatively compact attacker-controlled input. The technique works in both directions: many small headers can create one accordion pattern, while a large dynamic-table entry followed by repeated one-byte references creates the opposite pattern. FoxIO reportedly found this flaw class in seven of ten tested HTTP/3 stacks; suggested mitigations include disabling HTTP/3 in mitmproxy and setting a reasonable max\_field\_section\_size in the h3 crate, while IIS, Envoy, and ngtcp2/nghttp2 were listed as unaffected in the search results.

**Background**: QPACK is HTTP/3’s header-compression format, designed to accommodate the out-of-order stream delivery characteristics of QUIC. It can avoid repeatedly transmitting identical header values, but that economy means a compact representation may still trigger substantial work on the server. In this research, the reported impact includes memory amplification and potential denial of service against affected HTTP/3 implementations.
