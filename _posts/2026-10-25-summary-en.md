## [Personalized mRNA Vaccine Cuts Melanoma Recurrence Risk by More Than 40%](https://www.wired.com/story/new-melanoma-vaccine-reduces-risk-of-tumor-reoccurrence-by-over-40-percent/) ⭐️ 9.0/10

A patient-specific mRNA cancer vaccine reduced melanoma recurrence risk by more than 40% in a Phase 3 clinical trial. The result provides late-stage clinical evidence for combining a tumor-tailored vaccine with existing melanoma immunotherapy. This milestone suggests that mRNA technology can support genuinely personalized cancer treatment rather than only preventive vaccination. If confirmed and approved, the approach could improve outcomes for melanoma patients at high risk of recurrence and establish a model for targeting tumor-specific mutations in other cancers. The vaccine is customized to the mutation profile, or neoantigen fingerprint, of an individual patient’s tumor. The reported result comes from a Phase 3 study in resected stage IIB–IV melanoma, where intismeran autogene, also known as V940 or mRNA-4157, was evaluated with pembrolizumab against pembrolizumab alone; longer follow-up and regulatory review are still important before drawing broader conclusions.

rss · Wired · Oct 24, 11:30

**Background**: Cancer vaccines train the immune system to recognize tumor-specific targets. Neoantigens are peptide fragments created by tumor-specific somatic mutations and are therefore attractive candidates for personalized treatment because they can distinguish malignant cells from healthy tissue. Melanoma has been an important testing ground for neoantigen-based vaccines, and mRNA platforms can be used to encode a selected set of these targets for immune activation.

## [A 10-Year-Old CNN Outperforms Frontier LLMs on Skin Cancer](https://lynchlab.net/llm-skin-cancer) ⭐️ 8.0/10

An Inception v3 convolutional neural network, originally introduced around a decade ago and fine-tuned on 68,011 clinical and dermoscopic images from the ISIC Archive, reportedly outperformed tested frontier multimodal language models on 200 held-out skin lesions. The evaluation reported that patient- and lesion-related images were removed from training and validation, and the threshold was not selected using the test cases. The result challenges the assumption that larger, general-purpose multimodal models are automatically better at narrowly defined visual classification. It supports using language models primarily as tools for generating data, explanations, or workflows while deploying specialized computer-vision models for high-stakes domain tasks. The reported model used an Inception architecture pretrained on everyday photographs, with all layers fine-tuned on dermatology data using a single H100 GPU. The test involved only 200 lesions and appears to compare directly trained specialist models with general-purpose models without equivalent dermatology-specific tuning, so it should not be interpreted as proof that CNNs universally outperform modern multimodal systems.

hackernews · Hacker News \(热门\) · Oct 25, 17:15 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50233779)

**Background**: Convolutional neural networks learn hierarchical visual features directly from images, and the Inception architecture uses modules with multiple convolutional filter sizes to capture patterns at different scales. Transfer learning adapts a pretrained network to a specialized dataset, a common approach in medical-image analysis where labeled clinical images can be relatively limited. Multimodal large language models combine visual and textual processing, offering broader reasoning and zero-shot capabilities but often without the specialization of a model explicitly fine-tuned for one diagnostic task.
