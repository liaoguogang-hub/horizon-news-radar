## [Rust Enables Next-Generation Trait Solver on Nightly](https://blog.rust-lang.org/2026/08/21/enabling-next-solver-on-nightly/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The Rust team announced that the next-generation trait solver has been enabled by default on the nightly compiler channel, after nearly four years of development. They plan to stabilize it in the coming months, following a period of nightly testing to surface any remaining issues. The Rust blog describes this as the largest single change to the Rust compiler since its initial release. It fixes long-standing type system unsoundnesses, improves compile times, and unblocks future language features that depend on a more capable trait solver. The new solver replaces the legacy select-and-fulfill implementation with a goal-based proof model that recursively proves nested obligations against candidate sources such as user impls and param-env bounds. Multiple known unsoundnesses are blocked on this new solver, so enabling it is a prerequisite for resolving them.

rss · Lobsters \(技术社区\) · Aug 21, 15:15

**Background**: In Rust, a trait is a mechanism for ad-hoc polymorphism, similar to interfaces in other languages. The trait solver is the compiler component responsible for resolving trait obligations — for example, determining whether a given type implements a required trait, or which impl to select when multiple candidates exist. The existing implementation, based on a select-then-fulfill pipeline, has accumulated bugs, inefficiencies, and architectural limitations over the years. The next-generation solver is a from-scratch reimplementation designed to address these issues and provide a foundation for future language evolution. Rust&\#x27;s nightly channel is where experimental changes land before being promoted to beta and then stable.

## [Epigenetic Aging Clocks Tested as Clinical Trial Surrogate Endpoints](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04524-1) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A landmark Nature Medicine study led by Steve Horvath systematically tested whether epigenetic aging clocks meet the criteria for valid surrogate endpoints in clinical trials, evaluating 16 different DNA methylation clocks across 51 longitudinal studies of pharmacological and lifestyle interventions in humans. If epigenetic clocks can be validated as surrogate endpoints, they could dramatically accelerate and reduce the cost of clinical trials for longevity interventions by providing a faster, measurable signal of clinical benefit—transforming regulatory pathways for aging-targeted therapies. The study assessed responsiveness of clocks to genuine interventions and stability when no intervention occurred, covering a broad pharmacological and lifestyle range. Key clocks tested include Horvath&\#x27;s original clock, GrimAge, and DunedinPACE, each measuring different facets of biological age.

rss · Nature Medicine · Aug 21, 00:00

**Background**: Epigenetic clocks are analytical tools that estimate biological age by measuring age-related DNA methylation patterns across the genome, rather than relying on chronological age. Developed by Steve Horvath and others, clocks such as GrimAge and DunedinPACE aim to capture the pace of aging and health status more accurately than birthdate alone. A surrogate endpoint in a clinical trial is a biomarker used in place of a hard clinical outcome \(like death or disease onset\); to qualify, it must reliably respond when a treatment works and remain stable when a treatment is ineffective—the benchmark this new study set out to test.

A researcher accidentally logged hundreds of thousands of phone call routing queries to military bases by operating an e164.arpa ENUM nameserver, revealing that legacy telephony infrastructure is still surprisingly active. This discovery raises serious security concerns about legacy telephony infrastructure, showing that sensitive call routing data can be passively captured due to outdated protocols like ENUM that never achieved widespread public adoption but remain in use behind the scenes. The e164.arpa domain uses DNS NAPTR records to map E.164 telephone numbers to internet addresses. Although ENUM was largely abandoned for public use, it persists in private telecommunications contexts such as number porting lookups via VPNs, meaning the researcher inadvertently tapped into active but non-public telephony infrastructure.

hackernews · Hacker News \(热门\) · Aug 21, 13:11 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49387570)

**Background**: ENUM is an IETF-developed protocol designed to map the international public switched telephone network \(PSTN\) numbering system with Internet addressing, using DNS NAPTR records under the e164.arpa domain. While ENUM was envisioned as a way to unify telephony and the internet in the early 2000s, it never gained widespread public adoption. The Austrian telecommunications regulator was notably forward-thinking about SIP and ENUM-based services during that era. Today, ENUM largely operates as a private overlay infrastructure used by telecom operators for number porting and routing rather than as a publicly accessible service.

A detailed analysis has been published outlining planned compile-time improvements for the upcoming LLVM 23 release, covering various optimizations aimed at reducing build times across the compiler infrastructure. rss · Lobsters \(技术社区\) · Aug 22, 06:37

**Background**: LLVM \(Low Level Virtual Machine\) is a collection of modular and reusable compiler and toolchain technologies that originated as a research project at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign. The article was published online on 21 August 2026 in Nature Medicine \(doi:10.1038/s41591-026-04588-z\) as a perspective piece rather than a primary research study, meaning it synthesizes ongoing challenges and efforts in the field rather than presenting new experimental data.

**Background**: Traditional medicine encompasses diverse practices such as Traditional Chinese Medicine \(TCM\), Ayurveda, and various indigenous healing systems that have been used for centuries across different cultures. The WHO has long recognized the role of traditional medicine in global health and has previously issued strategies to promote its safe and effective integration. However, incorporating these practices into evidence-based modern medicine requires rigorous clinical trials, standardized quality control, and clear regulatory frameworks to ensure patient safety and proven efficacy.
