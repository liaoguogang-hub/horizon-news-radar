## [Levetiracetam targets GABAergic synapses in diffuse midline glioma](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04646-6) ⭐️ 9.0/10

A study published in Nature Medicine on September 17, 2026, demonstrates that the antiseizure drug levetiracetam selectively dampens GABAergic synaptic signaling to reduce tumor growth and extend survival in diffuse midline glioma \(DMG\). The therapeutic effect operates through an SV2A-independent mechanism, distinguishing it from levetiracetam&\#x27;s canonical antiseizure action, and was validated in both experimental models and patient cohort data. This finding is significant because it identifies an FDA-approved drug that can be immediately repurposed for a devastating pediatric brain cancer with extremely limited treatment options, potentially offering a rapid path to clinical benefit. It also advances the emerging field of cancer neuroscience by demonstrating that neuronal activity—specifically GABAergic signaling—can be therapeutically targeted to influence tumor progression. Crucially, the antiglioma effect was specific to diffuse midline glioma and was NOT observed in hemispheric high-grade glioma, suggesting tumor-type-selective vulnerability to GABAergic modulation. The SV2A-independent mechanism represents a novel pharmacological pathway distinct from levetiracetam&\#x27;s known antiseizure action via the synaptic vesicle glycoprotein 2A.

rss · Nature Medicine · Sep 17, 00:00

**Background**: Diffuse midline glioma \(DMG\) is an aggressive grade 4 brain tumor that primarily affects children and is notoriously resistant to treatment, with a relative five-year survival rate of approximately 42.2%. Levetiracetam is a widely used antiseizure medication whose canonical mechanism involves binding to SV2A, a synaptic vesicle protein involved in regulating neurotransmitter release. GABAergic signaling refers to inhibitory neurotransmission mediated by gamma-aminobutyric acid \(GABA\), which regulates neuronal excitability throughout the brain. Recent work in cancer neuroscience has revealed that neurons and glial tumors engage in active crosstalk, and modulating neurotransmitter signaling can influence tumor growth.

## [FDA Approves First Gene Therapy for Sanfilippo Syndrome Type A](http://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-approves-first-gene-therapy-pediatric-patients-sanfilippo-syndrome-type) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The FDA approved Fayuvi \(rebisufligene etisparvovec-hopf\), the first-ever gene therapy for pediatric patients with mucopolysaccharidosis type IIIA \(MPS IIIA\), also known as Sanfilippo syndrome type A. Developed by Ultragenyx, this one-time AAV9-based gene therapy delivers a functional copy of the SGSH gene to patients&\#x27; cells. This approval represents a landmark milestone for MPS IIIA patients and their families, as there were previously no approved treatments for this devastating, fatal neurodegenerative disease. It also demonstrates the growing maturity of AAV-based gene therapy for rare pediatric CNS disorders, potentially paving the way for similar approaches to other lysosomal storage diseases. The therapy uses an AAV9 viral vector to deliver a functional SGSH gene, targeting the root cause of the disease rather than just managing symptoms. Dosing is limited to a single infusion per lifetime at no more than 3 × 10¹³ vector genomes per kilogram of body weight.

rss · FDA Press Releases \(国际\) · Sep 17, 18:26

**Background**: Sanfilippo syndrome type A \(MPS IIIA\) is a rare autosomal recessive lysosomal storage disease caused by mutations in the SGSH gene, which lead to deficient degradation of heparan sulfate. The resulting accumulation of glycosaminoglycans \(GAGs\) causes rapid neurodegeneration beginning in early childhood. The disease primarily affects the central nervous system, leading to severe cognitive decline, and is ultimately fatal. Gene therapy using adeno-associated virus \(AAV\) vectors has emerged as a promising strategy for monogenic CNS disorders, as AAV9 in particular is capable of crossing the blood-brain barrier to deliver therapeutic genes to neural tissue.

The study was published in Nature Medicine on September 16, 2026, and was conducted across four countries. Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma has one of the lowest survival rates among major cancers, largely because most cases are diagnosed at advanced stages when curative surgery is no longer feasible. PANXEON evaluates three pancreatic cancer biomarkers and applies AI to compute a composite risk score; the ongoing prospective trial is registered as NCT06388967. **Background**: A liquid biopsy is a blood-based test that detects signs of cancer, such as circulating tumor cells, tumor DNA, or exosomes — small vesicles released by cells. Carbohydrate antigen 19-9 \(CA 19-9\) is a glycoprotein routinely used as a tumor marker for gastrointestinal cancers, especially pancreatic cancer, though it lacks sufficient sensitivity for early-stage detection when used alone. Combining CA 19-9 with multi-analyte exosome profiling and AI-driven scoring represents a newer strategy designed to overcome the limitations of single-marker screening.

## [Researcher Recovers US Driver&\#x27;s License Barcode Signing Keys](https://ryan.science/blog/keys-not-included) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Security researcher Ryan recovered the cryptographic signing keys used by multiple US states to sign the PDF417 barcodes on driver&\#x27;s licenses, exposing critical flaws in the ID verification ecosystem. The investigation demonstrates that these signatures do not cover the cardholder&\#x27;s photo, meaning a valid barcode can be combined with any photo to forge a convincing fake ID. This vulnerability undermines the primary cryptographic mechanism that businesses, banks, and age-verification systems rely on to authenticate US driver&\#x27;s licenses. Because the signatures omit biometric data, even legitimate barcode signatures cannot detect photo substitution, effectively reducing the security of these checks to whatever anti-fraud processes exist outside the barcode itself. The AAMVA barcode standard encodes over 80 fields of personal data signed with ECDSA keys, but the signature covers only textual fields—not the printed photo or any biometric reference. The researcher extracted signing keys by analyzing publicly available card images and reverse-engineering the verification process, demonstrating that forging a barcode that passes cryptographic validation is feasible with real identity data.

hackernews · Hacker News \(热门\) · Sep 17, 03:03 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49735930)

**Background**: US driver&\#x27;s licenses carry a PDF417 barcode on the back, defined by the AAMVA \(American Association of Motor Vehicle Administrators\) standard, which encodes personal information such as name, date of birth, and address. Many modern versions also include a digital signature to allow scanners to cryptographically verify that the data has not been altered. Unlike biometric passports, which embed a digitally signed photo in the chip, US driver&\#x27;s licenses rely on a separate printed photograph that is not part of the signed payload. This architectural gap means that signature verification only confirms the textual data is authentic, not that the physical card matches the person presenting it.
