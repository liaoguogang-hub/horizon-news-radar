## [Building a Linux GPU Driver for the M4 Mac Mini in One Month](https://codyho.dev/blog/gpu-driver/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A developer has documented the process of reverse-engineering and building a working Linux GPU driver for the Apple M4 Mac Mini within just one month. The effort covers detailed technical challenges involved in getting the GPU operational on Linux on Apple&\#x27;s latest silicon. M4 support is particularly significant because Apple has introduced new firmware-level obfuscation \(SPTM\) that has delayed even the established Asahi Linux project in bringing up M4 devices. A community-driven one-month achievement could accelerate broader Linux compatibility and demonstrate that M4 GPUs are approachable without waiting for upstream kernel work. The work relies on reverse-engineering Apple&\#x27;s proprietary GPU architecture, building on prior AGX research from the Asahi Linux community. rss · Hacker News \(热门\) · Sep 15, 19:30

**Background**: Apple Silicon Macs use custom GPUs that are not natively supported by open-source graphics drivers, requiring projects like Asahi Linux to reverse-engineer the hardware. Earlier generations \(M1, M2\) saw GPU acceleration land in Linux after years of work, including support for OpenGL and gaming applications like Quake3. The M4 generation introduced additional hurdles through firmware obfuscation in the Secure Processor Trust Module \(SPTM\), which complicates the reverse-engineering process that earlier chips did not present.

Open-source and open-weight models from various providers have rapidly closed the performance gap that once separated closed frontier systems from freely available ones. This finding fundamentally challenges the economic rationale for paying premium prices for closed AI APIs and could reshape enterprise AI procurement strategies, favoring self-hosted or open-weight deployments for most use cases. It also intensifies competitive pressure on frontier labs whose business models depend on sustained capability moats and raises important questions about the sustainability of massive AI infrastructure investments. The 4-to-1 cost-to-lead-time ratio \(5x cost for ~4 months advantage\) suggests that capability decay for frontier models is accelerating as open ecosystems mature, with Chinese-developed models like DeepSeek, GLM, and Kimi alongside Western open models \(Gemma, Nemotron\) collectively compressing the gap. The report implies that for non-cutting-edge applications, waiting 4 months for open alternatives to catch up may be far more cost-effective than paying premium inference costs.

rss · Ars Technica · Sep 15, 12:00

**Background**: Frontier AI models refer to the most advanced large language models available at any given time, typically developed and hosted by major commercial labs as proprietary, API-accessible services. Open-source or open-weight large language models release their trained weights publicly, allowing anyone to download, run, fine-tune, or audit them, often at dramatically lower inference costs than commercial APIs. The capability gap between proprietary frontier models and open alternatives has been a central question in AI economics, as it determines whether organizations can rely on cheaper open models or must pay for cutting-edge commercial systems to remain competitive.

## [Donated Livers Can Be Made Biologically Younger](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/09/14/1144010/donated-livers-can-be-made-biologically-younger/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Researchers have developed a technique to rejuvenate donated livers, reducing their biological age and potentially extending their usable window for transplantation. The method aims to counteract the rapid degradation that occurs once an organ is removed from a donor&\#x27;s body. This breakthrough could dramatically expand the pool of viable donor livers, addressing the critical shortage that leaves many patients waiting for transplants. If the biological age of organs can be reliably reversed, marginal or older-donor organs that would normally be discarded could become transplantable, saving more lives. Currently, donated livers are typically flushed with preservative solution and stored on ice, with surgeons having only a matter of hours before the organ degrades too much for transplantation. The rejuvenation approach builds on advances such as normothermic machine perfusion \(NMP\), which maintains organs at body temperature with a blood-like supply rather than using static cold storage.

rss · MIT Technology Review · Sep 14, 16:11

**Background**: Liver transplantation is a life-saving procedure, but demand far exceeds the supply of donor organs. Traditional preservation relies on static cold storage, which limits how long an organ remains viable. Normothermic machine perfusion is an emerging alternative that recreates physiological conditions to better preserve organs and is being tested in clinical trials. The concept of biological age—distinct from chronological age—is measured using biomarkers such as telomere length, and reducing biological age in organs is a novel frontier in transplant medicine.

## [KRAS G12D inhibitors show early promise in advanced cancers](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04610-4) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Two early-phase clinical studies published in Nature Medicine report encouraging anti-tumor activity from selective KRAS G12D inhibitors in patients with advanced solid tumors harboring G12D mutations, including non-small cell lung cancer and pancreatic cancer. One of the studies evaluated GFH375 \(VS-7375\), an oral dual ON/OFF-binding KRAS G12D inhibitor, in a Phase 1 trial. KRAS G12D is the most common KRAS mutation across carcinomas, accounting for roughly 33% of pancreatic cancers and significant fractions of colorectal, appendiceal, and ampullary cancers, meaning a successful G12D-targeted therapy could benefit a far larger patient population than current KRAS G12C inhibitors. This represents a major step in overcoming the long-standing reputation of RAS oncogenes as &\#x27;undruggable&\#x27; and signals meaningful expansion of precision oncology. GFH375 binds KRAS G12D in both the active GTP-bound \(ON\) and inactive GDP-bound \(OFF\) states, a dual mechanism intended to achieve more complete pathway inhibition. In the Phase 1 trial, most treatment-related adverse events were low-grade, but one treatment-related death was reported, and encouraging response rates were observed in both non-small cell lung cancer and pancreatic cancer cohorts.

rss · Nature Medicine · Sep 15, 00:00

**Background**: KRAS is a GTPase that acts as an upstream regulator of the MAPK signaling pathway and is mutated in roughly one-third of all human cancers, making it one of the most important oncogenic drivers. For decades, RAS proteins were considered &\#x27;undruggable&\#x27; until the 2021 approval of sotorasib, the first KRAS G12C inhibitor. KRAS G12D, the most prevalent KRAS mutation across carcinomas, has been harder to address because it lacks the reactive cysteine residue that G12C inhibitors exploit. The recent FDA approval of daraxonrasib, a pan-RAS\(ON\) inhibitor, in August 2026 for pancreatic cancer marked the first RAS-targeted therapy approved for that disease, setting the stage for allele-specific approaches like G12D inhibitors.

## [Phase 2 RIO Trial: bNAbs Delay HIV Viral Rebound After Treatment Interruption](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04644-8) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The phase 2 RIO trial demonstrated that the broadly neutralizing antibodies \(bNAbs\) 3BNC117-LS and 10-1074-LS delayed viral rebound during analytical treatment interruption \(ATI\) in adult males living with HIV, and that the initial sensitivity of the viral reservoir to autologous antibodies was associated with longer time to rebound. These findings advance HIV cure research by identifying a predictive biomarker \(reservoir antibody sensitivity\) for bNAb efficacy and supporting the use of combination bNAb immunotherapy as a strategy to achieve transient viral control without daily antiretroviral therapy. The study used an LS \(leucineserine\) Fc modification to extend antibody half-life, and secondary outcomes revealed viral resistance to the bNAbs emerged in some participants, highlighting both the promise and limitations of bNAb monotherapy/combination approaches for functional cure.

rss · Nature Medicine · Sep 15, 00:00

**Background**: Broadly neutralizing antibodies \(bNAbs\) are antibodies capable of neutralizing many HIV-1 strains by targeting conserved sites on the virus. 3BNC117 targets the CD4 binding site, while 10-1074 targets the V3 glycan supersite; both have been engineered with the LS mutation to prolong their in vivo half-life. Analytical treatment interruption \(ATI\) is a clinical trial design in which participants temporarily stop antiretroviral therapy \(ART\) under close monitoring, allowing researchers to assess whether an experimental intervention can control viral rebound. The RIO trial, led by Michel Nussenzweig&\#x27;s group at The Rockefeller University, is part of a broader effort to develop immune-based strategies for an HIV functional cure.
