## [Pig Kidneys Cryopreserved for Months Revived Successfully](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/10/15/1146091/pig-kidneys-that-were-frozen-for-a-year-have-been-brought-back-to-life/) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Scientists have successfully cryopreserved pig kidneys at -150°C for months and rewarmed them to full function for the first time. The team demonstrated that whole kidneys can be stored in a glass-like vitrified state and later restored to working condition. This breakthrough could revolutionize organ transplantation by enabling long-term banking of organs, eliminating the race against time during organ transport. If replicated with human organs, it could dramatically expand the donor organ pool, enable global organ sharing, and save countless lives. The kidneys were vitrified—cooled to a glass-like state without ice crystal formation—using a specialized solution at -150°C. The critical challenge was achieving sufficiently fast and uniform rewarming rates to avoid devitrification and ice nucleation, which can damage tissue; the team overcame a technological barrier that had long hindered whole-organ cryopreservation.

rss · MIT Technology Review · Oct 15, 08:43

**Background**: Cryopreservation is the process of freezing biological material to preserve it for extended periods, typically at -80°C or in liquid nitrogen at -196°C. Vitrification is a specific technique that cools tissue so rapidly that it enters a glass-like, amorphous state without forming damaging ice crystals. While cooling large organs via vitrification has been achieved, the rewarming step has been the major bottleneck—critical warming rates must be even faster than cooling rates to prevent ice formation during thaw, which had made whole-organ cryopreservation impractical until now.

## [Big Fish: Exploiting a 37-Year-Old Kernel Bug in OpenBSD](https://calif.io/research/big-fish) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Security researcher Calif.io published a technical writeup titled &\#x27;Big Fish&\#x27; detailing the exploitation of a 37-year-old integer overflow bug in the OpenBSD kernel, tracing the flaw back to the original BSD source code and demonstrating a proof-of-concept exploit that gains root access by chaining two kernel flaws. This bug survived decades of auditing in OpenBSD, one of the most security-focused operating systems widely regarded as the most secure by default. Its persistence raises important questions about the limits of manual code review and the need for complementary techniques like fuzzing and static analysis to find deep logic flaws in kernel code. The main integer overflow bug dates back about 37 years to the original BSD source code and was apparently accidentally patched in August 1999 during a code rewrite in NetBSD, yet persisted in OpenBSD until now. The exploit chains two kernel flaws to escalate privileges to root, and the writeup explicitly discusses whether fuzzing or static analysis could have realistically caught this class of bug.

hackernews · Hacker News \(热门\) · Oct 16, 14:44 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50118977)

**Background**: OpenBSD is an open-source Unix-like operating system renowned for its security-first development philosophy, including proactive features like W^X, ASLR, and pledge/unveil. Kernel vulnerabilities are especially severe because they allow attackers to bypass most userspace protections and gain full control over a system. Integer overflow bugs occur when arithmetic operations produce values too large for the assigned data type, causing unexpected wraparound behavior that attackers can exploit. Despite OpenBSD&\#x27;s reputation for rigorous manual auditing, complex logic bugs in kernel subsystems can remain hidden for decades.

## [PRIMA Retinal Implant Restores Sight in Macular Degeneration Patients](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/10/16/1145840/prima-implant-restores-sight-macular-degeneration-patients/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The PRIMA retinal implant system, developed to restore vision in patients with age-related macular degeneration \(AMD\), was approved in Europe in July and is currently awaiting FDA clearance in the United States. The system pairs a tiny sub-retinal microchip with camera-equipped eyeglasses, enabling patients with advanced AMD to read and perform tasks such as solving crosswords again. More than 5 million people worldwide suffer from retinal atrophy caused by AMD, a leading cause of irreversible vision loss for which no restorative treatment previously existed. PRIMA represents the first technology capable of actually returning functional central vision to these patients, potentially transforming the standard of care for a previously untreatable condition. The PRIMA implant is a 2×2mm light-powered microchip containing 378 pixels that convert light signals from the external camera-equipped glasses into electrical stimulation of the remaining retinal cells. Clinical multi-center trials demonstrated that the majority of participants regained the ability to read, though the system only restores central vision and still requires users to wear the specialized AR glasses at all times.

rss · MIT Technology Review · Oct 16, 09:00

**Background**: Age-related macular degeneration \(AMD\) is a progressive eye disease that damages the macula, the central part of the retina responsible for sharp, detailed vision needed for reading and recognizing faces. In its advanced \(atrophic\) stage, AMD causes irreversible loss of central vision while typically sparing peripheral vision, and it most commonly affects older adults. The PRIMA system works by bypassing the degenerated photoreceptors: a camera in the glasses captures the visual scene, a near-infrared projector sends the image wirelessly to the sub-retinal implant, and the chip&\#x27;s pixels electrically stimulate the still-functional bipolar cells downstream in the retinal circuitry.

## [Phase 1 Trial of D2C7-ATC in Recurrent Glioma Shows Promise](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04715-w) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A phase 1 trial published in Nature Medicine reports that D2C7-ATC, an antibody-toxin conjugate targeting EGFR and EGFRvIII, demonstrated encouraging safety and efficacy signals in patients with recurrent malignant glioma. Translational analyses of patient samples also revealed intratumoral and systemic immune activation following treatment. Recurrent malignant glioma, particularly glioblastoma, remains one of the most lethal and treatment-resistant cancers, with very few therapeutic advances over the past two decades. A targeted therapy that not only delivers a cytotoxic payload but also triggers immune activation could address a major unmet need and potentially reshape the treatment paradigm for brain tumors. D2C7-ATC is built on a dual-targeting single-chain antibody \(scdsFv\) scaffold fused to PE38KDEL, a truncated Pseudomonas exotoxin, enabling it to simultaneously address tumors expressing wild-type EGFR, EGFRvIII, or both. Earlier preclinical and clinical work from Duke demonstrated that direct intratumoral delivery via convection-enhanced delivery \(CED\) is feasible, and this phase 1 study \(NCT04547777\) extends those findings with mechanistic immune activation data.

rss · Nature Medicine · Oct 16, 00:00

**Background**: Glioblastoma is the most aggressive primary brain tumor, and recurrence after standard chemoradiation is nearly universal; median survival remains roughly 12–15 months. EGFR is amplified or overexpressed in roughly half of glioblastomas, and a constitutively active mutant variant called EGFRvIII is found in about 25–30% of cases, making EGFR an attractive therapeutic target. Antibody-toxin conjugates \(sometimes called immunotoxins\) marry the tumor specificity of an antibody with a potent bacterial-derived toxin, in this case a truncated Pseudomonas exotoxin A fragment. Unlike traditional antibody-drug conjugates that deliver chemotherapy payloads, these agents directly kill tumor cells upon internalization and can release tumor antigens that stimulate an immune response.
