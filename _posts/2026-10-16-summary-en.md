## [Exploiting a 37-Year-Old Kernel Bug in OpenBSD](https://calif.io/research/big-fish) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Security researcher Calif has published a detailed write-up titled &\#x27;Big Fish&\#x27; describing the exploitation of a 37-year-old vulnerability in the OpenBSD kernel, demonstrating that long-lived obscure bugs can survive even in heavily audited codebases. The write-up covers the bug discovery, exploit development, and mitigation bypasses, including bypassing OpenBSD&\#x27;s KARL kernel address randomization by leveraging deterministic memory allocators. This research challenges the assumption that mature, continuously audited operating systems are free of old vulnerabilities, and underscores how certain kernel-level logic flaws can evade human reviewers, fuzzers, and static analyzers for decades. It has implications for all long-lived OS codebases \(Linux, BSD derivatives\) and reinforces the need for layered mitigations, since the bug also survived in NetBSD until an accidental rewrite in 1999. A key technical finding is that OpenBSD&\#x27;s KARLI kernel address re-linking randomizes function and data addresses, but the boot-time and best-fit memory allocators remain deterministic, providing a stable exploit primitive. The vulnerability was reportedly accidentally fixed in NetBSD in August 1999 during a code rewrite, meaning it persisted silently in OpenBSD&\#x27;s fork for over two more decades after its divergence.

hackernews · Hacker News \(热门\) · Oct 16, 14:44 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50118977)

**Background**: OpenBSD is a security-focused, free Unix-like operating system forked from NetBSD 1.0 by Theo de Raadt in 1995, and its project has maintained a dedicated security auditing team of six to twelve members since 1996. The OpenBSD kernel is monolithic and inherits the 4.4BSD/NetBSD Unix kernel architecture, exposing services to software via system calls. OpenBSD is widely regarded as among the most security-proactive BSD variants, integrating exploit mitigations like KARL \(Kernel Address Randomized Link\), privilege separation, signed updates, and conservative defaults—making the survival of a 37-year-old bug especially noteworthy.

## [CGPM Adopts Resolution to Eliminate Leap Seconds by 2035](https://www.bipm.org/en/cgpm-2026/resolution-5) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The General Conference on Weights and Measures \(CGPM\) has adopted a resolution to eliminate leap seconds from Coordinated Universal Time \(UTC\) by 2035. The change will end the irregular one-second adjustments that have historically kept atomic time aligned with Earth&\#x27;s rotation. This is a historically significant decision in timekeeping that will impact global computing infrastructure, satellite navigation systems, and telecommunications, many of which have long struggled to handle unpredictable leap seconds. It effectively ends more than five decades of practice and shifts UTC toward a purely continuous atomic time standard. Before discontinuing leap seconds entirely, the resolution introduces a period during which a divergence of up to one minute between UTC and the time scale based on Earth&\#x27;s rotation will be tolerated. This interim phase allows affected systems and stakeholders to adapt before the final cutover to a continuous reference time.

rss · Lobsters \(技术社区\) · Oct 15, 20:22

**Background**: Coordinated Universal Time \(UTC\) is the primary global time standard, first officially adopted in 1963 and adjusted via leap seconds starting in 1972. Leap seconds are irregular one-second adjustments inserted into UTC to compensate for the fact that Earth&\#x27;s rotation is not perfectly uniform, while atomic clocks provide extremely stable timekeeping based on quantum transitions. The CGPM \(Conférence Générale des Poids et Mesures\) is the general conference of the International Bureau of Weights and Measures \(BIPM\), which together oversee international metrology standards and make decisions on measurement-related matters that affect the world.

## [Pig kidneys frozen for a year revived, advancing organ banking](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/10/15/1146091/pig-kidneys-that-were-frozen-for-a-year-have-been-brought-back-to-life/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Scientists have successfully cryopreserved pig kidneys at -150°C, stored them for months to a year, rewarmed them, and demonstrated that the organs regained function. This marks the first time whole organs of this scale have been revived after such extended ultra-cold storage. This breakthrough addresses one of the most critical bottlenecks in transplant medicine: the extreme time pressure and logistical constraints that limit how far organs can travel and how long they remain viable. If extended to human organs, it could enable true organ banking, dramatically increasing transplant availability and saving many lives. The kidneys were preserved using vitrification, a process that solidifies biological material into a glass-like state without forming damaging ice crystals. Cryopreservation of whole organs remains experimental—unlike single cells—and preventing ice crystal damage at the scale of a whole kidney has been a longstanding technical challenge. The kidneys used were similar in size to human kidneys, suggesting translational potential.

rss · MIT Technology Review · Oct 15, 08:43

**Background**: Cryopreservation is the process of freezing biological materials at extremely low temperatures—typically below -150°C—to halt all cellular activity and prevent decay. Vitrification is a specialized cryopreservation technique that avoids the formation of ice crystals by converting tissue directly into a glass-like solid state, which is critical because ice crystals can severely damage cell membranes and structures. While cryopreservation of individual cells \(such as eggs, sperm, and embryos\) has been routine for decades, scaling the technique up to whole organs like kidneys has remained an unsolved challenge due to their size, complexity, and susceptibility to freeze-related damage during both cooling and rewarming.

## [JPEG XL vs. AVIF: A Deep Dive Comparison for Web Image Formats](https://jakearchibald.com/2026/jpeg-xl-vs-avif/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Jake Archibald published an in-depth technical comparison of JPEG XL and AVIF for web use, examining encoding speed, file size efficiency, perceptual quality differences, and decoder behavior across various image types including photos, screenshots, and illustrations. Choosing the right modern image format directly impacts page load times, visual fidelity, and bandwidth costs for websites. As neither JPEG XL nor AVIF has achieved universal browser support, this comparison helps developers make informed decisions about which format to adopt based on their specific use case and quality requirements. The article reveals that libjxl&\#x27;s maximum effort encoding produces files 2–7% smaller than default for photos and ~25% smaller for screenshots/illustrations, but takes 50–180x as long \(9–46 seconds\). One notable criticism is that AVIF&\#x27;s perceptual tuning can erase and replace fine details \(like car highlights\) with plausible-looking artifacts, a flaw that becomes obvious once noticed.

hackernews · Hacker News \(热门\) · Oct 16, 16:12 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50120224)

**Background**: JPEG XL is a royalty-free raster image format designed to outperform JPEG, PNG, WebP, and JPEG 2000 in compression efficiency while supporting both lossy and lossless compression as well as transparency and animation. AVIF \(AV1 Image File Format\) is a newer format based on the AV1 video codec, also royalty-free, and supported in Chrome and Opera since 2020, Firefox since 2021, and Safari since 2022. Both formats aim to deliver superior quality at smaller file sizes than legacy formats like JPEG and PNG, but they differ significantly in encoding speed, decoder maturity, and browser support coverage, making the choice between them a nuanced decision for web developers.

The research explores how these widely used cryptographic parameters were originally chosen and documented. This research is significant because ECDSA curve parameters underpin much of modern internet security, yet the historical provenance of certain parameters has long been a subject of curiosity and occasional suspicion within the cryptography community. Using AI to assist in cryptographic historical research could open new methodologies for investigating the origins of foundational security infrastructure. ## [Conversational AI Lets Patients Tune Epilepsy Seizure Detectors](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04719-6) ⭐️ 6.0/10

A study published in Nature Medicine on October 16, 2026, demonstrates that patients can fine-tune their own seizure detection devices by conversing with an AI agent. The researchers found that the more significant remaining challenge is not the conversational interface but ensuring that the AI produces appropriate and reliable responses in clinical contexts. This represents a novel patient-centered approach to medical AI that empowers individuals to personalize their own neurotechnology, potentially improving seizure management outcomes. If the approach scales, it could shift implantable device calibration from clinician-only workflows toward shared patient-clinician control. The study is small in scale, and the authors explicitly acknowledge that building AI responses that are contextually appropriate and trustworthy in medical settings remains the core unsolved problem. Patient-driven fine-tuning of seizure detectors introduces both personalization benefits and risks of misconfiguration that require safeguards.

rss · Nature Medicine · Oct 16, 00:00

**Background**: Seizure detection algorithms typically rely on physiological signals such as electroencephalography \(EEG\), electromyography \(EMG\), or electrocardiography \(ECG\) to classify time-series data as either ictal \(during a seizure\) or interictal \(between seizures\). Many of these algorithms are embedded in implantable devices that must be calibrated to a patient&\#x27;s specific neural and muscular patterns. Conversational AI agents have been increasingly explored in healthcare for tasks like symptom triage and patient communication, but applying them directly to the tuning of neurodevices is a recent frontier. The work joins a growing field of human-in-the-loop AI systems, where AI drafts suggestions but clinicians or patients retain oversight.

## [Digital Platform Delivery of GLP-1 Therapy Matches Trial Results in Real World](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04687-x) ⭐️ 6.0/10

A real-world study published in Nature Medicine analyzed 8,127 women in the UK who initiated GLP-1 receptor agonist therapy through a digital platform, finding mean weight reduction at 6 months comparable to results from randomized clinical trials. This finding supports the viability of digital-first care models for obesity treatment, potentially expanding access to GLP-1 therapies while reducing healthcare costs. It addresses a critical knowledge gap about whether tightly controlled trial outcomes translate into routine telehealth practice. The study is observational rather than randomized, which introduces potential selection bias since participants self-selected into the digital platform. The cohort was limited to women in the UK, so generalizability to men and other populations remains uncertain.

rss · Nature Medicine · Oct 16, 00:00

**Background**: GLP-1 receptor agonists are a class of drugs that mimic the natural GLP-1 hormone to regulate appetite and blood sugar, currently approved for type 2 diabetes and obesity. Randomized controlled trials \(RCTs\) are considered the gold standard for evaluating drug efficacy due to their rigorous design, but real-world evidence \(RWE\) studies examine how treatments perform in routine clinical settings with broader patient populations. Digital health platforms for GLP-1 delivery have grown rapidly, combining telehealth consultations with home delivery of medications, but until now their outcomes had not been rigorously validated against trial benchmarks.
