## [Matrix Multiplication’s Optimal Exponent Is Proven to Be 2.25](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/preprints/The-matrix-multiplication-exponent-is-nine-fourths-November-24-2026/paper.pdf) ⭐️ 9.0/10

The paper establishes that multiplying two n×n matrices over any field requires Ω\(n^2.25\) arithmetic operations, matching the already-known O\(n^2.25\) upper bound. It therefore proves that the matrix-multiplication exponent over every field is exactly 9/4. This closes a fundamental gap between lower and upper bounds in algebraic complexity, resolving the conjecture that the optimal exponent is 9/4. It also settles the matrix-multiplication problem on the cited Top 500 Open Problems list, although it does not immediately produce practical software. The decisive new contribution is the matching Ω\(n^2.25\) lower bound; the O\(n^2.25\) algorithm was already known and is reportedly far from practical. The Θ notation means that the result provides both lower and upper bounds that grow at the same asymptotic rate, rather than merely repeating the earlier upper-bound result.

hackernews · Hacker News \(热门\) · Nov 25, 01:19 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50637220)

**Background**: The matrix-multiplication exponent ω is the smallest real number for which two n×n matrices can be multiplied using O\(n^\(ω+o\(1\)\)\) field operations. Naive multiplication takes Θ\(n^3\), while Strassen’s method established a subcubic bound and later tensor-based algorithms improved it further. Determining the exact exponent is a central question at the intersection of algebra, algorithms, and complexity theory.

## [Paper Claims a Strongly Polynomial Linear Programming Solution](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/preprints/Strongly-polynomial-linear-programming-November-24-2026/paper.pdf) ⭐️ 9.0/10

The preprint claims to solve Smale’s ninth problem by presenting a strongly polynomial algorithm for linear programming. If validated, the result would resolve a long-standing open question in optimization and complexity theory. A strongly polynomial algorithm would make the arithmetic-operation count depend only on the dimensions of an LP instance, rather than on the numerical magnitude of its input values. Such a breakthrough could reshape theoretical algorithm design and improve the formal efficiency guarantees of linear-programming solvers. The work is presented as a preprint and the supplied material describes its result as a claimed solution, so independent verification and expert scrutiny remain essential. Prior strongly polynomial algorithms are known for fixed-dimensional linear programs and certain structured classes, but not for unrestricted instances.

rss · Hacker News \(best\) · Nov 25, 02:03

**Background**: Linear programming optimizes a linear objective subject to linear constraints and is foundational in optimization, economics, logistics, and computing. A strongly polynomial algorithm bounds the number of arithmetic operations by a polynomial in the problem dimensions and is independent of the bit lengths of the input numbers, unlike weakly polynomial methods. Smale’s ninth problem asks whether such an algorithm exists for general linear programming.

## [Check Point Management Servers Under Active Intrusion](https://www.greynoise.io/blog/check-point-security-management-servers-intrusion-campaign) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Attackers exploited a Check Point security management server by collecting JavaScript Math.random outputs, reconstructing the V8 PRNG state, and recovering the weak session-cookie signing key. They then forged administrator session cookies and used the forged sessions to compromise exposed management servers. The campaign shows that an exposed management server can be compromised even when its session cookies appear signed and otherwise difficult to forge. Successful administrative access may enable further intrusion, configuration changes, or remote code execution, making internet-facing management systems a high-value target. The described attack chain uses repeated requests to the HFS loginSrp1 endpoint to obtain session-cookie sid values generated with Math.random, reconstructs the V8 xorshift128+ state, and derives the signing key needed to forge a valid administrator cookie. The research identifies Rejetto HFS, rather than Check Point’s own software, as the vulnerable product embedded in the affected management server; the community also questioned the initial access vector, including whether CVE-2026-16232 or internet exposure enabled exploitation.

hackernews · Hacker News \(热门\) · Nov 24, 16:32 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50630594)

**Background**: JavaScript Math.random is designed for general-purpose pseudo-randomness rather than cryptographic security, and the V8 implementation used by Node.js can expose enough consecutive outputs for its internal state to be reconstructed. A signed session cookie normally protects against modification by using a secret signing key that clients cannot know. Search results also describe the underlying Rejetto HFS issue as CVE-2026-61500 and state that forged administrator sessions can lead to full administrative access and remote code execution through the server\_code configuration feature.
