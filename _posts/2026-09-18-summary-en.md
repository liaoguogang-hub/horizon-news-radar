## [Photon-Emission-Guided Laser Fault Injection Bypasses RP2350 Secure Debug](https://donjon.ledger.com/blog/rp2350-secure-debug-laser-fault-injection/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Ledger Donjon researchers have demonstrated a novel photon-emission-guided laser fault injection attack that successfully bypasses the secure debug protections on Raspberry Pi&\#x27;s RP2350 microcontroller. The technique combines photonic side-channel analysis to identify sensitive regions of the silicon die with precisely timed laser pulses to induce faults at critical moments. This research has significant implications for embedded systems security, as the RP2350 is widely adopted in commercial and hobbyist applications. It demonstrates that even modern microcontrollers with hardware-level secure debug features remain vulnerable to sophisticated fault injection techniques, raising concerns for manufacturers relying on these protections. The attack leverages quiescent photon emissions—light emitted by transistors during switching—to identify the precise locations on the RP2350 die where security-critical operations occur, enabling targeted laser fault injection with high precision. This combination of non-invasive side-channel analysis with invasive fault injection represents an advancement over traditional LFI techniques that require more guesswork in target selection.

rss · Hacker News \(热门\) · Sep 18, 16:54

**Background**: The RP2350 is a 32-bit dual-core microcontroller released by Raspberry Pi Ltd in August 2024, featuring selectable ARM Cortex-M33 and RISC-V \(Hazard3\) cores, designed with enhanced security features including secure debug protection. Laser Fault Injection \(LFI\) is a hardware attack technique that uses precisely focused infrared laser pulses to cause specific transistors to malfunction during critical operations. Photon Emission Analysis is a non-invasive side-channel technique that captures light emitted by transistors during switching, which can reveal circuit operations and data-dependent information.
