## [Devtools Must Be Open Source for LLM Customization](https://blog.exe.dev/devtools-must-be-open-source) ⭐️ 7.0/10

A blog post argues that developer tools must be open source so that large language models can directly modify hard-coded behavior in source code, rather than relying on config files, plugin systems, or user-facing settings. The author proposes using an automated cron job to fetch upstream changes and rebase local LLM-driven modifications nightly. This argument challenges decades of software design conventions — config files, plugin APIs, and settings panels — by suggesting LLMs can bypass them entirely and edit code as the customization layer. If the approach gained traction, it would reshape how open-source maintainers prioritize readability, how end users customize tools, and how much compute and electricity is spent on routine personalization. The author&\#x27;s flagship example suggests using an LLM to change a text editor&\#x27;s hard-coded font size by downloading, modifying, and rebuilding the source — a workflow that critics call highly inefficient and electrically wasteful compared to a one-line config change. Critics also warn that nightly automated rebases performed by an unreliable actor \(the LLM\) risk silently breaking user workflows, since automated functional checks cannot fully verify subjective behavior or regressions.

hackernews · Hacker News \(热门\) · Aug 3, 14:15 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49156111)

**Background**: Open-source software has long promised users the freedom to inspect and modify the code they run, but in practice most users — including experienced programmers — rely on community contributors rather than editing source themselves. Software customization has historically been abstracted into config files, command-line flags, and plugin or extension systems precisely because reading and patching source is too costly for most users. With the rise of LLM coding assistants such as GitHub Copilot, Cline, and Continue, editing source code on a user&\#x27;s behalf has become far more feasible, which is the premise this blog post builds on.

## [The Dunning-Kruger Effect Is Probably Not Real](https://www.mcgill.ca/oss/article/critical-thinking/dunning-kruger-effect-probably-not-real) ⭐️ 7.0/10

A critical analysis published by McGill&\#x27;s Office for Science and Society questions the validity of the Dunning-Kruger Effect, arguing that methodological flaws and failed replications undermine the original findings by David Dunning and Justin Kruger. The Dunning-Kruger Effect has become a cultural touchstone frequently invoked in tech, education, and online discourse to explain why unskilled people overestimate their abilities. If the effect does not hold up to scrutiny, it challenges how we discuss competence, metacognition, and self-assessment in both academic and everyday contexts. The article points to replication concerns and statistical methodology criticisms common in psychology meta-analyses, suggesting that the original effect sizes may not be reproducible. A 2023 reevaluation published in Thinking Skills and Creativity similarly reassesses the DK effect&\#x27;s empirical foundation.

rss · Hacker News \(热门\) · Aug 3, 19:39

**Background**: The Dunning-Kruger Effect, named after social psychologists David Dunning and Justin Kruger, was first described in 1999 and refers to a proposed cognitive bias in which people with low ability in a task overestimate their competence, while high-ability individuals tend to underestimate theirs. The original research focused on logical reasoning, grammar, and social skills, and subsequent studies extended the claim to domains such as medicine, driving, and education. However, psychology as a discipline has faced a broader &\#x27;replication crisis,&\#x27; in which many high-profile findings have failed to be reproduced reliably, raising questions about the validity of numerous established effects. Meta-analyses—statistical syntheses of multiple studies—have become a key tool for evaluating whether original effect sizes hold up across different samples and conditions.

rss · Nature Medicine · Aug 3, 00:00

**Background**: Antibody-drug conjugates \(ADCs\) are a class of targeted cancer therapeutics comprising three core components: a monoclonal antibody that targets a tumor-associated antigen, a potent cytotoxic small-molecule payload, and a chemical linker that joins them. The goal is to deliver the cytotoxic agent selectively to cancer cells, combining the specificity of antibody targeting with the potency of chemotherapy while reducing systemic toxicity. Advances in linker chemistry, including improvements in plasma stability and drug-to-antibody ratio \(DAR\) control, have been central to the recent growth of approved ADCs in oncology. Regulatory bodies such as the FDA have issued guidance to help sponsors navigate the unique clinical pharmacology challenges ADCs present, including their complex metabolism and distribution.
