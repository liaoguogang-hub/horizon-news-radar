The multicenter, randomized MILD-MT trial evaluated whether endovascular therapy plus best medical management improves outcomes in patients with mild acute ischemic stroke from anterior-circulation large-vessel occlusion. The New England Journal of Medicine report addresses whether thrombectomy benefits this patient population, for whom benefit had previously been uncertain. Thrombectomy is an established treatment for many acute ischemic strokes caused by large-vessel occlusion, but its value in patients with mild neurologic deficits has been unclear. The trial could influence clinical selection criteria, treatment guidelines, and access to neurointerventional care for patients who are still severely disabled by an arterial blockage. MILD-MT is a prospective, multicenter, randomized, open-label trial with blinded endpoint assessment, known as a PROBE design. It compares endovascular therapy plus best medical management with best medical management alone in mild ischemic stroke involving acute anterior-circulation large-vessel occlusion.

rss · NEJM · 最新文章 · Oct 24, 01:40

**Background**: Large-vessel occlusion occurs when a blood clot blocks a major artery supplying the brain, and endovascular therapy physically removes or retrieves the obstruction through catheter-based procedures. Mild stroke refers to a small initial neurological deficit, but the underlying arterial blockage can still cause extensive brain injury and disabling long-term outcomes. The MILD-MT protocol was designed as a multicenter, prospective, randomized, open-label, blinded-endpoint trial to address the uncertain benefit of thrombectomy in this group.

An ahead-of-print report in the New England Journal of Medicine presents an interim phase 3 analysis of ravulizumab in IgA nephropathy. In the I CAN trial, ravulizumab reduced proteinuria by week 10 and met the interim primary efficacy endpoint, which measures the change in urine protein-to-creatinine ratio at week 34. The findings indicate that terminal complement inhibition may address a clinically relevant pathway in IgA nephropathy, a serious kidney disease for which disease-specific treatments remain limited. If the final renal-function outcome confirms the interim signal, ravulizumab could become an important option for reducing proteinuria and slowing disease progression. Ravulizumab is a monoclonal antibody that binds complement protein C5, preventing its cleavage and reducing formation of the membrane attack complex. The trial&\#x27;s coprimary efficacy measures are urine protein-to-creatinine ratio at week 34 and estimated glomerular filtration rate at week 106, with interim and final analyses planned for those respective time points.

rss · NEJM · 最新文章 · Oct 23, 04:30

**Background**: IgA nephropathy involves immune-complex deposition in the kidneys, leading to proteinuria and progressive kidney dysfunction, while its pathogenesis remains incompletely understood. Evidence indicates that complement activation contributes to the disease, supporting the investigation of complement-targeted therapy. Ravulizumab inhibits terminal complement activation and is already used in other complement-mediated disorders, including paroxysmal nocturnal hemoglobinuria and neuromyelitis optica spectrum disorder.
