## [C\* Unifies C Programming and Verification](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02246) ⭐️ 7.0/10

C\* is a systems programming language and framework that integrates the development and verification of C programs. It aims to let programmers write and prove software in the same language, environment, and workflow rather than moving between separate programming and verification paradigms. C remains widely used in systems and safety-critical software, where subtle programming errors can have serious consequences. By reducing the separation between coding and proof, C\* could make formal verification more accessible, although its tooling maturity, scalability, and adoption in production remain open questions. The project is presented as a unified development-and-proof environment for C programs, addressing the disconnect that often makes verification less accessible and complicates real-time verification. The available material does not establish that C\* is production-ready or provide evidence of broad real-world adoption.

rss · Hacker News \(热门\) · Sep 8, 15:56

**Background**: Formal verification uses mathematical reasoning to show that software satisfies specified properties, rather than relying only on testing to detect defects. In systems programming, verification can be especially important because C code often interacts closely with hardware and low-level resources, but separate proof languages and tools can create a substantial barrier for developers.

A randomized, placebo-controlled trial published in The Lancet found that oral administration of the bacterial lysate OM-85 in early life did not reduce the incidence of wheezing lower respiratory tract illness in high-risk preschool children. The study concluded that bacterial lysates are not efficacious for the primary prevention of asthma-like symptoms during the preschool years. This negative finding is clinically important because OM-85 had been one of the most studied bacterial lysates for respiratory immune modulation, with prior evidence supporting its use in reducing recurrent respiratory infections. The result challenges the extension of bacterial lysate therapy from treatment settings into primary prevention of wheezing and asthma-like illness in young children. OM-85 works by stimulating Toll-like receptors on immune cells via pathogen-associated molecular patterns \(PAMPs\), activating monocytes, macrophages, dendritic cells, and natural killer cells. Despite this immunomodulatory mechanism and prior efficacy data in treating recurrent respiratory tract infections and COPD exacerbations, the trial showed no preventive benefit against wheezing in a high-risk pediatric population.
