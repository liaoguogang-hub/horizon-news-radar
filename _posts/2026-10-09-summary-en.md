## [Cloudflare acquires Deno with runtime end-of-life in one year](https://deno.com/blog/cloudflare) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Cloudflare has acquired Deno, the company behind the Deno JavaScript/TypeScript runtime created by Ryan Dahl. Following the acquisition, the Deno team will support the Deno runtime for one more year with monthly bug fix and security releases, after which they will end development of the runtime entirely, though it will remain open source for others to continue. This acquisition effectively ends Deno as an independent runtime and folds it into Cloudflare&\#x27;s edge computing strategy around Workers. It also reflects a broader wave of consolidation in the developer tooling space, as major companies acquire popular open-source JS projects \(e.g., Bun→Anthropic, Cursor→SpaceX, Astro/VoidZero→Cloudflare\), raising concerns about the long-term sustainability of community-driven developer tools. Deno is built on the V8 engine \(the same engine used by Chrome\) and written in Rust using Tokio for async I/O. Deno Deploy and Cloudflare Workers already had overlapping functionality as edge JavaScript runtimes, and existing documentation shows Deno code could already be deployed to Cloudflare Workers, suggesting the acquisition consolidates rather than expands Cloudflare&\#x27;s edge runtime capabilities.

hackernews · Hacker News \(热门\) · Oct 9, 13:03 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50019911)

**Background**: Deno was created in 2018 by Ryan Dahl, who had previously created Node.js, with the goal of fixing what he considered design mistakes in Node—particularly around security \(Deno runs scripts in a secure sandbox by default\), built-in TypeScript support, and a more modern module system. Over time Deno added npm compatibility to ease migration from Node, which some community members feel diluted its original vision. Cloudflare Workers is Cloudflare&\#x27;s serverless edge computing platform that runs JavaScript on Cloudflare&\#x27;s global network of data centers, competing with services like Deno Deploy.

## [2026 Nobel Prize in Chemistry Awarded for Asymmetric Synthesis Breakthrough](https://www.wired.com/story/life-is-asymmetric-the-scientists-who-figured-out-why-won-the-2026-nobel-prize-in-chemistry/) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Henri Kagan and Kensō Soai have been awarded the 2026 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for designing chemical reactions that produce only one mirror-image form of a molecule, known as an enantiomer. This recognition highlights a foundational advance in stereochemistry that is critical for pharmaceutical manufacturing, where producing a single enantiomer is often essential for drug safety and efficacy. The award underscores the growing importance of chirality control in modern chemistry and drug development. Asymmetric synthesis aims to produce a desired enantiomer selectively, avoiding the racemic mixture of equal parts left- and right-handed forms that traditional synthesis often yields. Chemocatalysis and biocatalysis are the two principal approaches used to achieve this enantioselectivity in active pharmaceutical ingredients \(APIs\).

rss · Wired · Oct 8, 10:30

**Background**: Chirality describes molecules that exist in two mirror-image forms, called enantiomers, much like left and right hands. While enantiomers share identical chemical formulas and bonds, their three-dimensional arrangements differ, often giving them distinct biological effects. Asymmetric synthesis is the technique of selectively producing one enantiomer over the other, which is crucial because many pharmaceuticals are chiral and only one form is therapeutically active, while the other may be inactive or even harmful.

## [Spinlocks Considered Harmful: Reassessing Busy-Wait Synchronization](https://matklad.github.io/2020/01/02/spinlocks-considered-harmful.html) ⭐️ 6.0/10

Developer matklad published a technical essay arguing that spinlocks are frequently misused in systems programming, and that OS-level synchronization primitives such as mutexes and futexes are generally preferable for most practical use cases. The post revisits the classic debate over when busy-waiting is appropriate versus when kernel-mediated blocking should be used. Choosing the wrong synchronization primitive can waste CPU cycles, hurt throughput, or introduce priority-inversion bugs in production systems. This guidance is particularly relevant for Rust and systems-language developers building high-performance concurrent libraries where lock-selection decisions directly affect latency and scalability. The essay draws a distinction between spinlocks \(which burn CPU by busy-waiting in userspace\) and kernel-level mutexes or futexes \(which park threads until the lock is available\). Hybrid mutexes that spin briefly before sleeping are highlighted as a common compromise that captures the benefits of both approaches on multi-core systems.

rss · Lobsters \(技术社区\) · Oct 9, 13:33

**Background**: A spinlock is a lock that a thread repeatedly polls in a tight loop until it becomes available, rather than yielding the CPU. This busy-waiting is efficient only when the lock is held for very short durations, because spinning wastes CPU cycles and can starve other threads. A mutex, in contrast, blocks the waiting thread via the operating system, so it consumes no CPU while waiting. On Linux, futex \(fast userspace mutex\) is the underlying mechanism that allows uncontended locks to be acquired entirely in userspace, while falling back to a kernel syscall only when threads must actually sleep, giving a hybrid fast-path/slow-path design. The classic guidance is to use spinlocks only when the critical section is extremely short and contention is rare, and prefer mutexes or futex-based locks everywhere else.
