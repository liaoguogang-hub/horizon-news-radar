## [OptimPacing Trial Tests Left Bundle-Branch Versus Right Ventricular Pacing](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2612972?ai=nejm&amp;af=R&amp;rss=currentIssue) ⭐️ 9.0/10

The multicenter OptimPacing randomized trial compared left bundle-branch pacing with right ventricular pacing in patients with atrioventricular block. Over a median follow-up of 35 months, left bundle-branch pacing was associated with a 59% lower risk of a composite of death, heart-failure hospitalization, or cardiomyopathy, prompting the trial to stop early for superiority. Right ventricular pacing remains the conventional treatment for atrioventricular block but can contribute to pacing-induced cardiomyopathy and adverse outcomes. The result could shift clinical practice toward more physiological left bundle-branch area pacing for patients who require long-term ventricular pacing. Participants were randomized to either pacing strategy; their mean age was 68 years, and approximately 58% were men. The reported benefit concerned a composite clinical outcome rather than a single endpoint, and the provided materials do not give confidence intervals, absolute event rates, adverse-event results, or full eligibility criteria.

rss · NEJM · 最新文章 · Nov 8, 09:30

**Background**: Atrioventricular block is a conduction disorder in which atrial electrical impulses are delayed or fail to reach the ventricles, often requiring permanent pacing. Left bundle-branch area pacing places the lead in the left septal region to activate the ventricles in a more physiological pattern, whereas right ventricular pacing activates the right ventricle first and may impair left-ventricular function over time.

## [E-means delivers near-Lloyd’s loss with roughly 10x speedup](https://e-means.github.io/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

E-means introduces an approximate k-means method based on exponential moving averages and mini-batch updates, reportedly reaching comparable loss to Lloyd’s algorithm in roughly one-tenth the epochs and wall-clock time. Reported results include about 10–45x GPU speedups on embedding workloads and a 146x GPU speedup in one community member’s IVF-index implementation. Faster convergence with comparable loss could substantially reduce the cost of large-scale clustering for applications such as retrieval, quantization, vector indexing, and data curation. The method is particularly relevant to GPU-based systems and billion-scale vector workloads, where minimizing passes over data is operationally important.
