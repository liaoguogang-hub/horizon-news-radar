## [Research Claims to Break the 1.58-bit Barrier for Ternary LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16338) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A newly published paper proposes a method that surpasses the widely cited 1.58-bit theoretical limit for ternary quantization in large language models. The work challenges the assumption, rooted in BitNet b1.58, that 3-valued weights \(\{−1, 0, +1\}\) represent the minimum information per parameter achievable without unacceptable accuracy loss. If validated, surpassing the 1.58-bit barrier could further shrink LLM memory footprints and inference costs, making powerful models deployable on even more resource-constrained hardware such as CPUs and edge devices. It would also open a new research direction in ultra-low-bit quantization, prompting re-examination of theoretical lower bounds. The paper is hosted on arXiv \(ID 2609.16338\) but the provided content is minimal, so specific technical mechanisms—such as whether the approach uses mixed-precision tricks, auxiliary scaling, or novel encoding schemes—are not yet verifiable from the available excerpt. Ternary quantization typically replaces multiplications with additions, so any sub-1.58-bit scheme would need to carefully manage how additional information is encoded per weight without reintroducing expensive operations.

rss · Hacker News \(热门\) · Sep 16, 20:59

**Background**: Quantization reduces the numerical precision of neural network weights to compress models and speed up inference. Ternary quantization restricts weights to three values \(−1, 0, +1\), achieving an average of log₂\(3\) ≈ 1.58 bits per weight. BitNet b1.58, popularized by Microsoft, demonstrated that LLMs trained natively with ternary weights can match full-precision performance while replacing multiplications with simple additions, enabling efficient CPU inference. The 1.58-bit figure has since been treated as a theoretical floor for this class of quantization.

EBNA-2 is a viral transcription factor that hijacks host cell machinery to regulate both viral and host gene expression, including genes at MS risk loci identified by genome-wide association studies.
