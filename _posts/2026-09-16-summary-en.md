## [Hackers Breached Flock Surveillance Cameras, Data Unencrypted](https://www.wired.com/story/hackers-flock-camera-data-shows-how-system-works/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Security researchers breached Flock Safety surveillance cameras deployed in public spaces, discovering that sensitive vehicle and license plate data was stored unencrypted on physically accessible devices. Flock&\#x27;s vulnerability disclosure policy \(VDP\) explicitly excludes any testing that involves interacting with the device or downloading its data, effectively discouraging meaningful security research. Flock cameras are widely deployed across the United States for automated license plate recognition \(ALPR\) and law enforcement use, meaning the exposed data affects public privacy and potentially compromises sensitive investigations. The combination of unencrypted local storage and a restrictive VDP signals a systemic failure to incorporate physical and adversarial threat models into public-safety IoT design. The breached camera logged roughly 3,300 vehicles per day with peaks of 4,454 and generated about 1.6 million images over 21 days, all recoverable from the device. Critics note the product lacks a proper secure boot architecture and adequate key management, largely because Flock relies on off-the-shelf hardware and software stacks that were not designed with physical-access threat models in mind.

**Background**: Flock Safety, founded in 2017, is a major U.S. vendor of automated license plate recognition \(ALPR\) cameras, video surveillance, and gunfire detection systems used by law enforcement and private organizations. These cameras are typically installed in unsecured outdoor locations such as utility poles and streetlights, where physical access by adversaries is a realistic possibility. A Vulnerability Disclosure Policy \(VDP\) is meant to invite security researchers to report flaws safely, but policies that prohibit interacting with the device or downloading its data render the policy largely performative and prevent the very research needed to uncover vulnerabilities.

Data centers themselves are physical facilities housing servers, storage, and networking equipment, supported by power, cooling, and fiber infrastructure. Fault tolerance and disaster recovery \(DR\) strategies in the cloud are typically designed around scenarios like hardware failures, software bugs, power outages, or natural disasters, not sustained military attacks.

## [EBV Reactivation Primes Immune System Before MS Relapse](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04665-3) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A study published in Nature Medicine demonstrates that increased expression of EBV reactivation genes in B cells, along with MS risk genes targeted by the viral protein EBNA-2, precedes multiple sclerosis relapses. This finding directly links viral reactivation and genetic susceptibility to the onset of MS attacks. This research provides a mechanistic explanation for how EBV, the strongest known environmental risk factor for MS, triggers relapses by reawakening latent infection and engaging MS-associated genetic loci. It strengthens the causal link between EBV and MS and opens avenues for antiviral or EBNA-2-targeted therapies to prevent relapses. The study integrates transcriptomic data from peripheral B cells with genetic risk mapping, showing that EBNA-2 preferentially binds to genomic intervals associated with MS risk. This convergence of viral transcription factor activity on host risk loci is allele-dependent, suggesting a gene-by-virus interaction that modulates disease activity.

rss · Nature Medicine · Sep 16, 00:00

**Background**: Epstein-Barr virus \(EBV\) infects over 90% of adults worldwide and establishes latent infection in B cells, occasionally reactivating to produce new virions. EBNA-2 is a key EBV transcription factor essential for B cell transformation and has been shown to bind genomic regions associated with autoimmune disease risk. Multiple sclerosis is a neuroinflammatory disease driven by immune attack on the central nervous system, and EBV infection is now considered a necessary but not sufficient risk factor. Understanding how EBV reactivation triggers MS relapses is a major goal in neuroimmunology.

## [Exosome liquid biopsy detects early pancreatic cancer in multi-country trial](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04625-x) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A prospective multi-country study of 1,785 individuals published in Nature Medicine on September 16, 2026, showed that the PANXEON exosome-based biomarker combined with CA 19-9 achieves high sensitivity for detecting early-stage pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma \(PDAC\). Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma is one of the deadliest cancers, largely because it is typically diagnosed at advanced stages when treatment options are limited. A non-invasive blood-based screening tool with high sensitivity for early-stage disease could dramatically improve survival outcomes and reshape clinical screening protocols. The study is registered as NCT06388967 and aims to prospectively validate an exosome-based miRNA signature for noninvasive early detection of PDAC. The combined PANXEON + CA 19-9 approach was evaluated across four countries, though independent validation and assessment of specificity and false-positive rates are still needed before clinical adoption.

rss · Nature Medicine · Sep 16, 00:00

**Background**: Liquid biopsy refers to analyzing tumor-derived material—such as circulating tumor DNA, exosomes, or microRNAs—from a simple blood sample, offering a non-invasive alternative to surgical tissue biopsies. Exosomes are tiny vesicles released by cells that carry molecular cargo including miRNAs, and exosome-based liquid biopsies leverage their stability for sensitive tumor detection. CA 19-9 \(carbohydrate antigen 19-9\) is the most widely used serum biomarker for PDAC management, but on its own it has low sensitivity for early-stage disease and is not expressed in individuals with certain Lewis-negative blood phenotypes, which has limited its utility as a standalone screening tool.

## [RIO Trial: bNAbs 3BNC117-LS and 10-1074-LS Delay HIV Rebound](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04644-8) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The phase II RIO trial reported in Nature Medicine showed that broadly neutralizing antibodies 3BNC117-LS and 10-1074-LS delayed viral rebound during analytical treatment interruption in adult males living with HIV. The study also found that initial sensitivity of the latent viral reservoir to autologous antibodies correlated with a longer time to rebound. This trial, led by prominent HIV immunologist Michel Nussenzweig, advances HIV cure strategies by demonstrating that bNAb-based interventions can meaningfully extend the period of viral suppression after stopping antiretroviral therapy. The reservoir-sensitivity finding suggests that personalized antibody approaches could improve outcomes in future cure-directed trials. The LS modifications on both antibodies \(3BNC117-LS and 10-1074-LS\) extend their in vivo half-life, enabling less frequent dosing. The study population was limited to adult males, and a key limitation is that not all participants showed reservoir sensitivity to the administered antibodies, which correlated with shorter rebound times.

rss · Nature Medicine · Sep 15, 00:00

**Background**: Broadly neutralizing antibodies \(bNAbs\) are rare antibodies that can recognize and neutralize a wide range of HIV strains, making them promising candidates for both prevention and cure research. Analytical treatment interruption \(ATI\) is a carefully monitored pause in antiretroviral therapy used in cure trials to evaluate whether an experimental intervention can delay or prevent viral rebound. The HIV latent reservoir, primarily residing in resting CD4+ T cells, persists despite ART and is the source of viral rebound when treatment stops. Recent research has shown that most reservoir viruses are resistant to a person&\#x27;s own autologous neutralizing antibodies, which is an important consideration when designing bNAb-based interventions.
