A perspective paper published in Nature Medicine on August 13, 2026, authored by Eric Xing and colleagues, proposes a conceptual framework for building an AI-driven digital organism \(AIDO\) composed of integrated multiscale foundation models to predict, simulate, and program biology at all levels. The proposed AIDO architecture consists of four layers: a data layer, a foundation model system layer, a downstream utility layer, and an applications layer for various bioengineering problems. The models are envisioned to be transformer-based or newer architectures, integrating multiple data types and biological constraints across molecular, cellular, tissue, and organismal scales.

rss · Nature Medicine · Aug 13, 00:00

**Background**: Foundation models are large-scale AI models trained on broad datasets that can be adapted to a wide range of downstream tasks, similar to how GPT models work for language. In biology, such models have been applied to individual domains like protein structure \(e.g., AlphaFold\) or genomics, but these efforts have largely remained siloed. Multiscale foundation models aim to bridge these gaps by integrating data across biological scales—from molecules to cells to whole organisms. The concept of an AI-driven digital organism extends this idea further, proposing a comprehensive computational system that can simulate and manipulate biological systems in silico, reducing the need for costly and time-consuming physical experiments.

## [Google Advances Practical Homomorphic Encryption for Private AI](https://blog.google/security/how-google-is-making-private-ai-practical-with-homomorphic-encryption/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Google has announced new efforts to make homomorphic encryption practical for private AI workloads, enabling computation on encrypted data without decryption. The announcement highlights techniques to reduce overhead and integrate HE into AI pipelines. If made truly practical, homomorphic encryption could allow organizations to outsource sensitive AI inference and training to cloud providers without exposing raw data, benefiting healthcare, finance, and other privacy-sensitive sectors. It represents a major step toward reconciling the data-hungry nature of modern AI with strict privacy requirements. Homomorphic encryption traditionally imposes computational overhead on the order of 1000x or more for inference tasks, and Google&\#x27;s announcement focuses on optimizations aimed at narrowing this gap. Practical viability remains constrained by noise growth in ciphertext, large memory footprint, and limited support for non-linear operations common in neural networks.

hackernews · Hacker News \(热门\) · Aug 14, 15:43 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49300314)

**Background**: Homomorphic encryption is a cryptographic technique that allows computations to be performed directly on encrypted data \(ciphertext\), producing an encrypted result that, when decrypted, matches the result of the same operations on plaintext. It enables privacy-preserving outsourced computation, for example allowing cloud services to process medical or financial data without ever seeing it in cleartext. However, HE schemes have historically suffered from massive computational and space overhead, limiting adoption to niche applications rather than general-purpose AI workloads.

Newer approaches based on the Semi-NCA algorithm by Georg et al. offer simpler proofs and competitive practical performance, though Lengauer-Tarjan is still widely taught and used.
