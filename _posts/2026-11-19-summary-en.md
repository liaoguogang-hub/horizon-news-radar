## [Critical NGINX HTTP/2 Flaws Enable Unauthenticated Remote Code Execution](https://www.wiz.io/blog/nginx-http2-rce-vulnerabilities) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Two critical NGINX HTTP/2 vulnerabilities, CVE-2026-123633 and CVE-2026-123007, were disclosed with a CVSS 4.0 score of 9.2 and can allow unauthenticated attackers to execute code when specified configuration conditions are met. Fixed versions 1.30.7 and 1.31.9 were released on November 18, 2026, and operators were urged to upgrade promptly or disable HTTP/2 as an interim mitigation. The flaws reside in NGINX itself, so secure application code cannot prevent exploitation of exposed HTTP/2 servers, potentially compromising websites, APIs, and sensitive data. Although HTTP/2 is not enabled by default in NGINX, it remains widely used, making the affected population substantial at Internet scale. Both vulnerabilities require HTTP/2 to be enabled; CVE-2026-123007 affects official releases dating back to NGINX 1.9.5 in 2015. Code execution reportedly requires ASLR to be disabled or bypassable, while the disclosed fixes are NGINX 1.30.7 and 1.31.9; Wiz has not published exploit code.

hackernews · Hacker News \(热门\) · Nov 18, 15:19 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50547374)

**Background**: HTTP/2 is a web protocol that improves transport efficiency over HTTP/1.1, but NGINX does not enable it by default and operators must explicitly configure it. A remote code execution flaw can let an attacker run arbitrary commands without credentials, and conditions such as a disabled or bypassable address-space layout randomization mechanism can materially affect exploitability. Upgrading to a fixed release is the durable response, while disabling HTTP/2 temporarily reduces exposure at the cost of protocol functionality.

## [FDA Approves First Therapy for GD3 Neurological Symptoms](http://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-approves-first-drug-treat-neurological-symptoms-type-3-gaucher-disease) ⭐️ 9.0/10

The FDA approved Infilbray \(venglustat\) tablets as the first treatment for neurological symptoms of Type 3 Gaucher disease in adults and pediatric patients weighing at least 15 kg, approximately 33 pounds. Venglustat is an oral, once-daily glucosylceramide synthase inhibitor developed by Sanofi. This approval addresses a major unmet need in a rare disease with progressive neurological damage and may provide affected patients with a disease-targeted oral option. It also marks the first FDA-approved therapy explicitly indicated for the neurological manifestations of Type 3 Gaucher disease. The approved population includes adults and children weighing at least 15 kg. Venglustat reduces the accumulation of harmful glycosphingolipids by inhibiting glucosylceramide synthase; however, the supplied announcement does not describe the clinical-trial endpoints, magnitude of neurological benefit, adverse effects, dosing adjustments, or other safety and efficacy details.

rss · FDA Press Releases \(国际\) · Nov 18, 19:13

**Background**: Gaucher disease is a rare lysosomal storage disorder caused by mutations in the GBA gene, and its manifestations vary by clinical type. Type 1 disease generally does not involve neurological damage, whereas Types 2 and 3 can include neurological impairment; chronic neuronopathic Type 3 disease is associated with progressive neurological damage. Venglustat targets the biochemical pathway underlying glycosphingolipid accumulation through inhibition of glucosylceramide synthase.

## [OpenAI Launches GPT-6.2 Astra and GPT-6.2 Sol](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/17/gpt62-astra-and-sol/) ⭐️ 9.0/10

OpenAI introduced two API models: GPT-6.2 Astra \(gpt-6.2-astra\), a new frontier model for hard projects priced at $8/MTok input and $40/MTok output with cached input at $0.40/MTok, and GPT-6.2 Sol \(gpt-6.2-sol\), a cheaper general-purpose model priced at $2/$10 per MTok, the same rate as 6.1 Sol and 6 Sol. Simon Willison shipped LLM 0.38 with support for the new models and published side-by-side output comparisons across effort levels. The 20% price cut on the frontier tier, plus a 60% drop in cached-input pricing, directly lowers the cost of long-running agentic sessions that repeatedly re-send large context windows, which is where cached-input billing dominates. GPT-6.2 Astra now matches Claude Fable 5.2 on price per token, sharpening competition at the top of the frontier-model market and giving developers a cheaper alternative for multi-step autonomous workflows. GPT-6.2 Astra costs 80% of the earlier GPT-6 Astra and GPT-6 Sol remains at half the price of GPT-5.6 Sol \($4/$20\); cached input for 6.2 Astra falls to $0.40/MTok from $1/MTok. In Willison&\#x27;s informal pelican-drawing and Three.js benchmark comparisons, the 6.2 models produced more detailed output at higher effort settings, but neither handled a complex Yonkers-mech animation as well as Claude Fable 5.2.

rss · Simon Willison \(AI 跨行业洞察\) · Nov 17, 22:38

**Background**: Frontier LLM APIs are typically billed per million tokens \(MTok\) across three tiers: standard input, cached input for repeated context, and output, with output tokens costing several times more than input. Because agentic workflows resend the same conversation context on every turn, cached-input pricing dominates the bill, which is why OpenAI&\#x27;s deep cut there matters more than the headline per-token price. The Astra/Sol split follows OpenAI&\#x27;s pattern of pairing a frontier model for demanding reasoning with a smaller, cheaper model for routine high-volume inference, and both models expose adjustable effort levels \(high, xhigh, max, and in Codex CLI, ultra\) that trade cost for quality.
