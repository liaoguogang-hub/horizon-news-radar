## [Voltage Glitching Unlocks A100 Memory in Mining Card](https://blog.kate.land/posts/using-voltage-glitching-to-unlock-the-a100/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A researcher used voltage fault injection to bypass firmware restrictions on a CMP 170HX mining card, unlocking memory normally disabled by the GA100 fuse controller and enabling use comparable to an A100-80G for accelerated AI workloads. The demonstrated method targets an 8 GB model and transforms it into a high-capacity accelerator. Repurposing low-cost mining hardware into high-memory AI accelerators could make large-model inference and other memory-intensive workloads more accessible. However, the method’s broader value depends on whether the glitch can be reproduced reliably, safely, and across other CMP models. The attack manipulates the GA100 fuse controller through precisely timed voltage glitches, with offset and width determining effectiveness. Physical fault injection carries risks including blown fuses or board damage, and community participants noted that other CMP cards, such as the 10 GB variant, may have different firmware behavior.

hackernews · Hacker News \(热门\) · Nov 11, 10:48 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50451903)

**Background**: NVIDIA derives datacenter A100 GPUs and CMP mining cards from the GA100 silicon design, with hardware fuses used to disable features for the mining-oriented product. CMP 170HX boards generally expose 8 GB of memory even though some related configurations have been associated with 80 GB dies, leaving much of the physical memory inaccessible to CUDA. Voltage glitching is a physical fault-injection technique that briefly perturbs a chip’s power supply to induce unintended behavior, such as skipping firmware checks or changing fuse-controlled configuration.
