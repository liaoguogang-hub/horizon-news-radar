## [Phase 1 Trial Shows WRN Inhibitor Targets MSI Cancers via Synthetic Lethality](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04693-z) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A phase 1 clinical trial published in Nature Medicine on September 29, 2026, reports that RO7589831, a first-in-class inhibitor of Werner syndrome helicase \(WRN\), demonstrated tolerability and partial responses in patients with microsatellite instability \(MSI\) and/or DNA mismatch repair-deficient solid tumors, with the maximum tolerated dose not being reached during dose escalation. This represents the first clinical proof-of-concept that WRN is a druggable synthetic lethal target in MSI cancers, offering a targeted therapy option for patients who do not respond to or cannot tolerate immune checkpoint inhibitors. It expands the precision oncology toolkit beyond existing biomarker-driven approaches such as PD-1/PD-L1 blockade. The trial tested RO7589831 in a dose-escalation design; while the maximum tolerated dose was not reached, some partial responses were observed, suggesting clinical activity but requiring larger cohorts to confirm efficacy. The approach exploits the fact that MSI-high tumors, which already have defective mismatch repair, become uniquely dependent on WRN helicase for DNA replication and repair.

rss · Nature Medicine · Sep 29, 00:00

**Background**: Microsatellite instability \(MSI\) arises when a cell&\#x27;s DNA mismatch repair \(MMR\) machinery is defective, leading to accumulation of frameshift mutations in short repetitive DNA sequences; MSI-high tumors are found in roughly 15% of colorectal cancers and a meaningful fraction of other solid tumors. Synthetic lethality describes a genetic interaction in which disrupting either of two genes alone is tolerable to a cell, but disrupting both together kills it—making it possible to selectively target tumor cells carrying a specific vulnerability while sparing healthy tissue. WRN \(Werner syndrome helicase\) is a RecQ family DNA helicase essential for resolving replication stress; because MSI-high cancer cells are already genomically unstable, they become critically dependent on WRN for survival, creating the synthetic lethal window exploited by RO7589831.

## [Oral Small-Molecule GLP-1 Agonist Safiglipron Shows Strong HbA1c Reduction in Phase 2/3 Trial](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04651-9) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The OUTSTAND-1 Phase 2/3 randomized double-blind placebo-controlled trial demonstrated that once-daily oral small-molecule GLP-1 receptor agonist safiglipron reduced HbA1c by up to 1.45% versus placebo over 32 weeks in people with early type 2 diabetes not yet on glucose-lowering medication. Body weight effects were described as modest. Oral small-molecule GLP-1 receptor agonists are a highly sought-after alternative to injectable peptide drugs like semaglutide \(Ozempic/Wegovy\), offering easier administration, potentially lower manufacturing costs, and improved patient accessibility. A clinically meaningful 1.45% HbA1c reduction represents substantial glycemic efficacy, though the modest weight loss effect may limit the drug&\#x27;s competitiveness in the obesity-focused GLP-1 market.
